AWARD 

This is a lefcrcnce under See. 10(1 )(d) of the Industrial 
Disputes Act 1947 (hereinafter called the Act). 

The question teferred to Is:- — 

"Whether the management of New Chirimiri Ponri Hill 
Colliery, Post Office Chirimiri, District Surguja 
(Madhya Pradesh), was justified in laying off the 



1791

Sec. 3(ii) ] 


46 workmen listed below from the 29th March, 1971 
to the 3rd April, 1971, deducting 6 days wages and 
causing break in service. If not, to what relief are 
the workmen entitled?” 

The schedule to the releicnce give* the names of the afore- 
said 46 workmen. 

It appears that, at first, theie was only one labour union 
viz. N. P. Colliery Workers Federation (hereinafter called the 
Federation) in the New Chirimiri Ponri Hill Colliery (here- 
inafter colled the Colliery) since 1955. In 1970, however, an- 
other Union viz. the M P. Koyala Mazdoor Panchayat (herein- 
after called the Panchayat) was formed and evidently theie was 
rivalry between the two Unions. It is alleged on behalf of 
the workmen that 46 workmen of the Colliery were laid off 
without compensation from 29-3-1971 to 3-4-1971 and their 
six days wages were deducted from their pay “as fine and 
punishment” and they were treated ns new hands after making 
breaks in their services. It has been urged on behalf of 
Ihe Colliery that for the aforesaid six days viz. 29-3-1971 to 
3-4-71 the woikers were on illegal strike and no work was 
done by them and therefore they weic not paid any wages for 
the aforesaid peiiod. ' It was categoi ically stated on behalf of 
the management that no break was made in the service of 
the aforesaid 46 persons. There is no evidence to substan- 
tiate the contention of the workmen that there was break in 
their service as was alleged by them. It was contended on 
behalf of the workmen that the management had declared u 
lock out and therefore the workmen were unable to perform 
their duties and they were entitled to their wages for the 
period in question. Sri Iiardeo Singh refuted the conten- 
tion of the management that the workmen had gone on 
strike. He categorically stated that there was no srike by 
the workmen. Ex. E/I dated 1-4-1971 is a letter addressed 
by the Panchayat to the Chief Labour Commissioner (Cen- 
tral), New Delhi. It said that there was spontaneous strike 
in the Colliery and it mentions the demand of the workmen. 
Ex, E/4 is a notice dated 29-3-1971 issued by Shri G. Sri- 
niwasan. Chief Mining Engineer under Section 22 and 
Rule 73 of the Act and Rules about the strike to the 
Assistant Labour Commissioner (Central) Shahdol. Ex. E/8 
is a letter dated 31-3-1971 by the President of the M. P. 
Colliery Workers Federation, Ponri Hill Colliery Branch 
addressed to the Manager of the Colliery. It enclosed a 
copy of a resolution passed by the Fedeiation in which it 
was said that the strike was illegal, Ex. E/9 are minutes 
of a joint meeting between the representatives of the 
management and the workmen as represented by the M.P. 
Colliery Workers Federation and the various sections of 
the Colliery workers. It was lecorded in (he minutes that 
the strike was totally illegal. It is clear from the evidence 
on the record that the workmen had struck work on 29-3- 
1971 and had continued to abstain from working till 
3-4-1971, A lay off uotice was given by the management on 
30-3-1971 because of the strike of loaders which had para- 
lysed the working in the colliery. It was considered neces- 
sary to lay off some workmen from whom work could not 
be taken on account of the prevailing strike, Shri Hardeo 
Singh who appeared on behalf of the workmen could not 
substantiate his contention that the management itself spread 
alarm among the workmen of the Colliery and had pre- 
vented the workmen from discharging their normal duties. 
Ex. E/17', is a significant document. It bears the thumb 
impressions of 79 workmen and it was addressed to the 
Manager of the Colliery. It was stated therein that the 
workmen had been misled in not joining their duties and 
they tendered their apologies to the management. Ii.W. 2., 
Shankar, was working as a Coal Dresser in the Colliery, 
He admitted that he had stopped working for six days viz, 
29-3-1971 to 3-4-1971. He further admitted that all the 
striking workers had received two notices from the manage- 
ment. In his cross-examination he Btated: — 

"I had gone to work on 29th, I had marked my 
attendance but my other fellow workers asked me 
to desist from work”. 

He adpiPled (hat he had put his thumb mark in the letter 
(Ex. E/16) but added that “the letter was not read over to 
me". E.W.3, Udio who works as a loader in the Colliery 
slated (hat there was J-fi days stiike in March /April 1971 
and he also did not work, In his cross-examination he 
stated that there was Halla and workmen struck work. He 
further stated : — 

“No labour leader had asked us to go on strike. There 
were about 400 or 500 workmen from different 


sections who were raising Halla. No fellow loader 
asked me to go on strike. I did not go to work 
because of the Halla”. 

He further stated that he had apologised and no deduction 
was made from his wages. W.W.l Banu Lai was working as 
a Loader in the Colliery. In his examination-in-chief he 
stated that he and his fellow workers were “laid off”. He 
further admitted that “no one stopped us from working. 
He admitted in his cross-examination that: — 

"There were 150 persons in my shift and all of them 
struck work on 29th March 1971. There was no 
work for five days". 

The evidence on the record makes it clear that the work- 
men themselves had struck work and the contention on their 
hehalf that they were laid off from March 29, 1971 has 
no substance. I have no hesitation in holding that there 
was no lay off by the management us was alleged on behalf of 
the workmen. The strike of the workmen was illegal and 
since they had absented themselves from work their six 
days wages had been lawfully deducted. My answer 
to the reference is that the 46 workmen mentioned in the 
schedule to the reference had not been laid off from 29-3-1971 
to 3-4-1971. Their six days wages had been lawfully de- 
ducted because they absented themselves from work and no 
break was made by the management in the service of the 
aforesaid 46 workmen. I make my award accordingly, f 
make no order for costs.
