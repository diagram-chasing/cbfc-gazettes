S.O. 531. — In exercise of the powers conferred by section 51 of the Indian 
Electricity Act, 1910 (9 of 1910) , as applied to the State of Pondicherry, the Cen- 
tral Government for the placing of electricity supply lines, appliances and 
apparatus for the transmission of energy, hereby confers upon the Director of 
Electricity, Pondicherry, all the powers which the telegraph authority possesses 
under the Indian Telegraph Act, 1805 (13 of 1885), as applied to that State, with 
respect to the placing of telegraph lines and posts for the purposes of a telegraph 
established or maintained by the Government or to be so established or maintain- 
ed. 

The powers hereby conferred shall be exercised by the Director of Electricity 
subject to — 

(1) the following conditions, namely: — 

(a) before the placing of appliances and apparatus for the transmission of 

energy across any private property, the Director of Electricity shall 
issue a notice to the affected party or parties calling for objections in 
writing to be filed with him within fourteen days of the receipt of 
the notice; 

(b) such notice may be sent to the party or parties by registered post or 

it may be served in person by a special messenger. Where none of 
these modes is found possible, the notice shall be published in the 
Pondicherry Official Gazette and a copy of it shall also be affixed on 
the notice board of the Maire concerned or in some other prominent 
place such as the village chavadi nearest to the place where the work 
is to be executed; 

(c) where no objections are received within the time specified, the work 

may be proceeded with straightaway; 

(d) where any objection is received, the Director of Electricity shall hold 

an enquiry into the objections raised and take a decision whether the 
objections should be accepted or over-ruled; 

(e) When appliances and apparatus for the transmission of energy are 

placed, every endeavour shall be made (i) to place the appliances 
and apparatus with minimum damage to the property and (li) wher- 
ever possible, to erect the poles on the bunds of fields instead of 
right in the middle of them; and 

(2) the provisions of the Indian Telegraph Act, aforesaid. ^ 08-GP/62]

AWARD 

This is a petition under Section 33-A of the Industrial Disputes Act. 

2. The petitioner alleges, that the opposite party has contravened Section 33 
of the Industrial Disputes Act, that the petitioner was appointed in the Bank of 
Raiasthan as Chowkidar, that he was confirmed as Chowkidar with effect from 
12th March, 1958, that his duties were changed to those of a peon by virtue of the 
office order dated 13th September, 1961, and that changing these duties amounts 
t.o contravention of Section 33. 


3. Tiie contention on behalf of the bank is, that the bank has not contravened 
any of the provisions of Section 33, that it is true, that the was appoi - 

ed as a Chowkidar. that he joined duty only from 12th June, 1957, that the Pa 
has not changed his designation, that change of duties does not mean change 
designation, that this apolication is not maintainable, that ther# is + 

of the conditions of service to the prejudice of the workman, and that ne is 
entitled to any relief. 


4. The issues, that arise for determination, are: — 

(1) Whether there is a contravention of Section 33 of the Industrial Dis- 

putes Act? 

(2) Whether the petition, as brought, is maintainable under Section 33-A, 

of the Industrial Disputes Act? , 

(3) Whether there is alteration of the conditions of service to the preju ce 

of the workman? 

(4) To what relief, if any, is the petitioner entitled? 


/sswes No, 1 and 2. 

5. It is common ground, that the petitioner was appointed as Chow l? r ,, 0 . t 
hank, Ext. W/t, is a letter of confirmation dated 3rd M arch, > ^ pc^g 

the effect, that he was confirmed in service with effect from It ’

Ext, M/1, is tho letter addressed to Shri Ganpat Singh by the manager of the 
bank. Ext. M/2, dated 9th September, 1961, is a copy of a letter by the manager 
of the Bank of Rajasthan, Jodhpur, addressed to the Central Office, stating, that 
the office needed peons, and that it was proposed that the duties of chowkidar be 
changed to those of a peon, that tho branch was situated in the heart of the city, 
and that there was no need of a chowkidar. The contention on behalf of the 
workman now is, that there has been an alteration of the conditions of service 
to his prejudice during the pendency of a dispute before the National Tribunal, 
and that, therefore, there is a contravention of Section 33, and that it follows, 
that the petition, as brought, is maintainable. This contention is untenable. The 
complaint of the petitioner is, that there has been an alteration of the conditions 
of his service, because he was confirmed as a chowkidar, and he is now made to 
do the duties of a peon, and that his designation also was changed. What is con- 
tended before me is, that there has been an alteration of the conditions of his 
service, which were applicable to him immediately before the commencement of 
the proceedings before the National Tribunal. But it is clear that Section 33 has 
no application to the facts of this case. Section 33(1) cannot appl y because the 
alteration of the conditions of service, that is now complained of, is not connected 
with the dispute before the National Tribunal. Neither can it be held, that Sec- 
tion 33(2) has been contravened because it is clear from the decision in Lord 
Krishna Textile Mills (1961, I LLJ 211), that a management is not bound to apply 
for approval of their action of altering the conditions of service of a workman. 
Section 33(3) has obviously no application when the petitioner is not a protected 
workman. In the circumstances, there is no contravention of Section 33 in 
the absence of contravention of Section 33, the petition under Section 33-A, is 
not maintainable, and this Labour Court has no jurisdiction to entertain the same. 


Issue No. 3. 

0. In view of my finding, as above, all questions relating to the merits of the 
dispute between the parties do not arise for determination, and are not decided 
herein. 


Issue No. 4. 

7. The petitioner is not entitled to any relief in this petition. 

8. In the result, the petition is dismissed as not being maintainable. There 
will be no order as to costs.
