Cost and Works Accountants Act: 

Almost simultaneously with the introducticn of the 
Chartered Accountants (Amendment) Bill in the Parlia- 
ment in 1958, the Government of India introduced the Cost 
ad Works Accountants Bill with a view to set up a sepa- 
rate Institute of Cost and Works Accountants on the same 
lines as the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India. 

The Council gave evidence before the Joint Committee 
of Parliament to which the Bill was referred for detailed 
consideration. 

It was represented to the Committee on behalf of the 
Council that it was premature to introduce the Cost and 
Works Accountants Bill at this stage as that branch of 
accountancy had not developed sufficiently in India to 
justify the creation of a separate statutory body for the 
purpose and that, in any case, if Government was anxious 
to develop that branch, the task could be entrusted to the 
Institute of Chartered Accountants of India. If, however, 
the Parliament wanted to set up a separate statutory 
body for the specialised study of a particular subject, there 
may not be any objection tp it by the profession. The 
objection of the profession was to the erroneous descrip- 
tion of a branch of the , accountancy profession as a 
distinct profession. It was emphasized that there was no 
'‘profession of Cost and Works Accountants” existing as 
such in the country. 

The Joint Committee was also made aware of the fear 
in the minds of the members of the Institute that the Bill 
might restrict the fundamental right of many of them to 
practise the profession of Chartered Accountancy which in 
fact includes Cost Accountancy. The feeling was voiced 
several times previously also and on each occasion the 
Government assured the Institute that there was no in- 
tention on the part of Government to take away the work 
of Cost Accountancy from Chartered Accountants. 

\ 

The Bill has now become Law and the Institute of Cost 
and Works Accountants has been set up. The Council, is, 
however, making every effort to see that the interests of 
the members of the Institute do not, in any way, suffer 
due to the creation of the Institute of Cost and Works Ac- 
countants.
