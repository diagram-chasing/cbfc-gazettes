$ . O . 2448. — In exercise of the powers conferred by the proviso to article 309 of 
the Constitution , the President hereby makes the following rules regulating the 
method of recruitment of persons to Class III posts in the Indian Agricultural 
Research Institute , New Delhi, namely : 

1 . Short title . - These rules may be called the Indian Agricultural Research 
Institute ( Class III ) Recruitment Rules, 1961, 

2 . Application . These rules shall apply to Class III (Ministerial and Non 
Ministerial ) posts in the Indian Agricultural Research Institute , New Delhi, in 
cluding its Regional offices and sub - stations as specified in column 2 of the Sche 
dule annexed hereto . 

3 . Classification and Scale of Pay. — The classification and the scale of pay 
attached to the said posts shall be as specified in columns 3 to 5 of the Schedule 
to these rulcs . 

4 . Method of recruitment, age limit and other quallfications. The method of 
recruitment to the posts aforesaid , age limit, qualifications, and other matters 
connected therewith shall be as specified in columns 6 to 11 of the Schedule 
aforesaid . Provided that the upper age limit prescribed for direct recruitment 
may be relaxed in the case of Scheduled Castes / Tribes candidates, displaced 
persons and other special categories in accordance with the orders issued from 
time to time by the Government of India . 

5 . Disqualification . -- ( a ) No person , who has more than one wife llving or 
who haying a spouse liying, mariics in any case in which such marriage is void 
by reason of its taking place during the life - time of such spouse , shall be eligible 
for appointment to service ; and 

( b ) no woman , whose marriage is void by reasons of the husband having a 
wife living at the time of such marriage or who has married a person who has 
a wife living at the time of such marriage, shall be eligible for appointment to 
service, provided that the Central Government may, If satisfied that there are 
special grounds for so ordering , excmpt any person from the operation of this

8 , 0 . 2450. — The following draft of Oil cakes Grading and Marking Rules, 1961 , 
which it is proposed to make, in exercise of the powers conferred by gection 3 of the 
Agricultural Produce (Grading and Marking ) Act , 1937, ( 1 of 1937) , is published , 
as requi ed by the said section for the information of all persons likely to be 
affected thereby and notice is hereby given that the said draft will be taken into 
consideration on or after 15th November, 1961. 

Any objection or suggestion which may be received from any person with 
respect to the said draft before the date specified , will be considered by the 
Central Government. 

1. Short title . — These rules may be called the Oil cakes Grading and Marking 
Rules, 1961. 
2 . Definition . - - In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires - 

( a ) Schedule means a Schedule annexed to these rules, 
and ( b ) Agricultural Marketing Adviser means the Agricultural Marketing 

Adviser to the Government of India , 
3 . Grade designations -- The grade designations to indicate the quality of oil 
cakes shall be as set out in column 1 of Schedules II to IV . 

4 . Definition of quality . The definition of quality indicated by the grade 
designations shall be as set out in columns 2 to 9 of Schedules II to IV . 

5 . Grade designation marks.- - The grade designation mark shall consist cf a 
label bearing the , design set out in Schedule I and specifying the grade designa 
tion . 

6 . Method of marking . - ( 1 ) The grade designation mark shall be securely 
affixed to each bag in a manner approved by the Agricultural Marketing Adviser . 
In addition to the grade designation mark , each bag shall be clearly marked with 
the following particulars and in such a manner as may from time to time be 
specified by the aforesaid officer 

( a ) name of packer , 
( b ) batch number , 
( c ) net weight in kilograms, and 

( d ) date of packing. 
( 2 ) An authorised packer may , after obtaining the previous approval of the 
Agricultural Marketing Adviser, mark his private trade mark on the bag in a 
manner approved by the said officer , provided that the private trade mark does 
not represent quality or grade of the oil cake different from that indicated by the 
grade designation mark alfixed on the package in accordance with these rules . 

7 . Method of packing.- Oll cakes shall be packed only in clean and sound jute 
bags. Each bag shall contain 75 kilograms of oil cake of only one type and of 
the same batch , 

8 . Special conditions of Certificate of Authorisation . In addition to condi 
tions specifled in rule 4 of the General Grading and Marking Rules , 1937, the follow 
ing conditions shall also be the conditions of every Certificate of Authorisation 
issued for the purpose of these rules : - 
( a ) An authorised packer shall take all precautions to avoid admixture of 

oil cakes with castor seed and castor cake. 
(b ) If an authorised packer handles more than one type of oil cake in the 

same premises, adequate precautions shall be taken by him to avoid 

mixing of different oil cakes. 
( c ) An authorised packer shall make such arrangements for testing oli 

cakes as may be prescribed from time to time by the Agricultural 
Marketing Adviser . He shall also maintain proper records of the 

analysis of the samples . 
( d ) All instructions regarding methods of sampling and analysis , 

packing and marking, and maintenance of records, which may be 
Issued from time to time by the Agricultural Marketing Adviser shall 
be observed ,

AWARD 
This is a petition under Section 33 - A , Qf the Industrial Disputes Act. 

2 . The petitioner alleges , that the Cochin Commercial Bank has contravened 
the provision of Section 33 , of the Act, that the petitioner while working as Agent 
of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank happened to mislay the key of the Cabi 
net in which the key of the safe was deposited , that after a shot while , the 
duplicate key was received , that the activities of the Bank were carried on as 
usual though there was a slight delay , that on 29th December , 1960, the General 
Manager suspended the petitioner , that on 28th February , 1961, the General 
Manager dismissed the petitioner with effect from 17th January , 1961, that the 
petitioner s appeal to the Board of Directors was unsuccessful, that the order of 
dismissal of the petitioner is illegal, that it should be set aside with suitable 
relief. 

3 . The contention on behalf of the Bank is , that the petitioner was an Agent 
of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank , that he was not a workman , that his 
duties were mostly manage: ial or administrative, that accordingly , this petition 
is not maintainable , and there is no contravention of Section 33 , that this Labour 
Court has no jurisdiction to adjudicate upon the petition , that on the merits , the 
dismissal of the petitioner is bona fide and lawful, that it cannot be interfered 
with , and that the petitioner should be dismissed , 
4 . The issues, that arise for determination are:-- - 

(1 ) Whether the petition as brought, is maintainable ? 
( ii ) Whether there is contravention of Section 33 of the Act ? 
( iii ) Whether the petitioner is a workman within the meaning of Section 

2 (s ) of the Act ? 
(iv ) Whether the dismissal of the petitioner is lawful and justified ? 
( v ) Whether the dismissal of the petitioner is wrongful and is not bona 

fide ? 
( vi) To what relief, if any, is the petitioner entitled ? 
Issues No. 1 - 3. 

5 . This is a petition under Section 33 - A , of the Industrial Disputes Act . The 
petitioner Shri K . M , Poulose was the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch of the 
Cochin Commercial Bank Limited , which has its head office at Cochin . A charge 
sheet was served against the petitioner , Ext. W / 1, dated 29th December , 1960 . 
The first charge was, that on 26th December, 1960 , as Agent of the Arakunnam 
Branch Mr. Poulose mislaid the key of the safe of the Bank , and was unable to 
meet the demands of parties for payment of money, when there was already a 
panic among the public about a rumoured moratorium , and also absented himself 
for half an hour from the Omce. The second charge was, that the petitioner 
kept the key of the safe in the office cabinet at night, which was contrary to 
office orders . The petitioner was directed to furnish his explanation within two 
days. Ext, M / 18, is a report dated 27th December, 1960, that was sent by Mr. 
Poulose to the Head Office. It is to the effect, that in his hurry to reach the 
Bank , he could not find out the key of the safe of the cabinet in his lodge, that he 
rushed to the Bank , thinking that the key might have been kept in the Bank 
itself, and that it was not found there, and that with the help of Mr. V . J . Kurien , 
he managed to get the duplicate key from the Head Office and resumed work , 
Subsequently he traced out the key in his lodge and retu - ned the duplicate key 
to the Head Office, The petitioner begged to be excused for the inconvenience 
caused in this connection . In reply to the charge - sheet Shri Poulose gave the 
explanation Ext. M / 19, dated 31st December , 1960 , and therein he referred to his 
report dated 27th December , 1960 . He admitted that he had no idea , that he was

doing a wrong thing , when he kept the key of the safe in the cabinet , and assur 
ed the Head Office, that he would be more careful in future, Ext. W / 2 , is a

that the enquiry into the charge framed against him , had been concluded , that 
he was examined on 5th January , 1961, that he was guilty of the charge framed 
against him , that he was guilty of serious mis - conduct, and that nothing short of 
dismissal would be adequate punishment. The petitioner was asked to show 
cause , why he should not be dismissed from the service of the Bank with effect 
from 17th January , 1961. Ext. W / 3 . is a letter dated 28th February , 1961, and

the Bank with effect from 17th January , 1961. Ext. M / 21, shows, that the peti 
tioner filed an appeal to the Board of Directors. The appeal wag however dis 
missed as shown by Ext. M / 22 , dated 25th Mauch , 1981. The petitioner s case is , 
that the order of dismissal passed by the Bank is unlawful, and unjustified and 
that he should be re - instated in service together with back wages . 

6 . The preliminary objection has been raised by the Bank , that there is no 
contravention of Section 33 of the Industrial Disputes Act , and that this Labour 
Court has no jurisdiction to entertain the present petition . However , the conten 
tion on behalf of the petitioner is, that there is a contravention of Section 33 
because no petition was fled for approval under Section 33 ( 2 ) ( b ) of the Indus 
trial Disputes Act and one month s wages were not paid to him as laid down in 
that Section . The management have admitted , that they did not Ale any applica 
tion under Section 33 ( 2 ) ( b ) for approval, and also did not pay one month s wages 
as required by that Section , but their answer is , that they were not bound to do 
so , because Mr. Poulose was not a workman within the meaning of Section 2 ( 3 ) 
of the Industrial Disputes Act, and Section 33 did no apply to him . If the con 
tention of the management is to be up -held , than it was not necessary for them 
to comply with the conditions laid down in Section 33 ( 2 ) ( b ) . 


7 . Thus the question for determination is , whether the petitioner is a " work 
man " as defined in Section 2 ( s ) of the Act. It is important to note , that he was 
the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank at the material time. The case 
of the Bank is , that his duties were mostly managerial and administrative as 
Agent of the Bank . It is well settled , that it is from the nature of the duties 
performed by an individual, that we must judge, whether an employee is a 
workman or not as defined in Section 2 ( s ) . It will be necessary to determine, 
whether looking to the nature of the duties performed by Mr. Poulose , it can be 
said , that he was a workman within the meaning of the Section . The General 
Manager of the Bank has been examined as MWI. According to him , the peti 
tioner Mr. Poulose , was an agent of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank , and not 
merely the Accountant or Accountant Incharge of the Branch . He was responsi 
ble for running the Branch . He had a power of attorney ( Ext . M / 2 ) in his 
favour, and he was exercising the powers conferred by the power of attorney . 
He was the Controlling Authority in the Branch . He controlled the Staff in that 
Branch . He granted casual leave to them . He was making confidential reports , 
about the Staff. He was making recommendations about increments and pronio 
tions of the Staff under him , and his recommendations were usually accepted by 
the Head Office. He could make on the spot decision in an emergency and report 
the matter to the Head Office . Mr. Poulose also deposited cash security as the 
other agents had to deposit. He had the discretion and power to grant over 
drafts , within the limits of accommodation contained in Ext, M / 5 . Mr. Poulose 
was accepting gold ornaments and advancing loans to customers after 
determining the limits of such loans. He was checking up the day - to 
day working of the Branch . He was enforcing discipline of the Branch . The 
correspondence in regard to the Branch with the Head Office , was 
carried on by him . He was accepting gold ornaments and advancing 
loans on them , Shri Poulose was getting Rs. 25 / - extra , because he was Agent. 
The Head Office was treating Shri Poulose as an agent and not as a clerk . Mr. 
Poulose was supervising the work of the Branch . He had to allot work to 
members of the staff. He was responsible for the securities and cash ot the 
Bank primarily . The securities and money of the Branch , are the responsibility 
of the Agent. though the cashier hag also got the key of the safe. Mr. Poulogn 
was responsible for the administration of the Arakunnam Branch . He had power 
to take disciplinary action against any member of the staff. He had power to 
operate acrounts in other branches of the Bank . In short the Agent s duty was 
to see that the work of the branch was carried on and he had power to enforce 
discipline. Though this was not specifically conferred , it was implied in the 
position of Mr. Poulose as Agent.

9 . On a consideration of the evidence on either side, it is established , that 
Mr. Poulose as agent of the Arakunnam Branch , was exercising duties mainly of 
a managerial and administrative character. He was the Administrative Head and 
Manager of the branch . He was in management of the entire affairs of the branch . 
He was the person Incharge of the branch . He was the person responsible to 
the Head Office for the affairs of the branch . He exercised control and super 
vision over the members of the staff, working under him , and he was even sending 
confidential reports with his recommendations about them about increments and 
promotions. He had discretion , initiative and power to grant loans and over 
drafts to customers of the Bank , though of course within the limits prescribed . He 
had also got the power to operate accounts of other branches of the Bank , and 
that is how he got Rs. 10 ,000 / - from another branch on 28th December 1960. It 
is not possible to hold , that he was a workman , doing mainly clerical duties. He 
was a person in a position of command and control of the branch at Arakunnam , 
and there was no one above him in that branch . It is important to note, that 
he was able to take on the spot decisions in an emergency . Thus even in respect 
of the incident, that took place on 26th December 1960, he got into contact with 
the Head Office , and other branches of the Bank , and got cash and the cash was 
received from the Molamthurthi Branch of the Bank . Shri Poulose admits , that 
he had to recommend grant of increments though he adds, that it was in accord 
ance with the sanction of the Head Office . He was granting casual leave. He 
was sending confidential reports on the forms supplied . He admits, that he sent 
Exts , M / 8 to M / 13. He also admits, that in the branch , in which , he was working , 
there was no person above him . 


10 . From the evidence , it is fairly clear , that Mr. Poulose was exercising duties 
mainly of a managerial and administrative nature. His duties were not manual 
or clerical as attempted to be argued on his behalt . It is , however , urged by 
Mr. T , C , N . Menon , Learned Counsel on behalf of the workman , that he was 
described not as Agent but as Accountant of Accountant- in - charge. Ext. W / 17 
has been relied upon for the purpose of showing , that Shri Poulose was described 
as Accountant- in -charge, but this is dated 4th November 1946 . Thar further shows, 
that he was appointed Agent with effect from 1st December 1946 of the Peru 
manoor Office . Nextly , attention is drawn to Ext. W / 4 , dated 19th December 
1953 to show , that Shri Poulose was an Accountant. But this was in the Tri 
vandrum branch , which was a big branch , and there he was only an Accountant. 
The same remarks apply to Ext. W / 5 and Ext. W / B which show , that Shri 
Poulose was Accountant in the Cochin Branch , Ext W / 8 , dated 4th October 
1955, describes him as Agent at Fort Cochin Branch , What is material is , that 
W / 10 proves , that Shri Poulose was placed Incharge of the Arakunnam Branch 
from 18th December, 1957, and he was Agent of that Branch since then . The 
contention on behalf of the petitioner that he was only an Accountant or officiat 
ing Accountant or Accountant- in -charge, and that this really shows, that he was 
not an Agent, is not entitled to acceptance . Whatever , he might have heer in 
the past , he was the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch from 1957, and at the 
material time in 1980 . It may be, that in the past he acted , as Accountant in 
big offices, like Cochin and Trivandrum , but what is more important and to the 
purpose is , that Shri Poulose was the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch in Decem 
ber , 1960 , and at the time of dismissal. He was in complete , sole and independent 
charge of the said Branch , as Agent and was discharging mainly managerial and 
administrative functions in running the business of the Branch . 


11. It is however , urged that Shri Poulose was only getting the pay . that was 
being paid to clerks in the clerical scale . It is also argued , that he was getting 
medical aid and Exts . W / 15 to 16 have been produced in this connection . It is 
also clear from the evidence , that he was getting Dearness Allowance , according 
to the scale prescribed in the Shastry Award. It is elicited in the cross -examina 
tion of the General Manager , that Agents are glven House Rent Allowance , but 
Mr, Poulose was not given House Rent Allowance . The Oficers not governed 
by the award were getting Axed D . A ., but Mr. Poulose was getting D . A ., accord . 
ing to the Shastry Award scale , at the time of dismissal. Omicers not governed

by the award , did not get medical assistance , but Mr. Poulose was given medical 
benefits . Even granting , that Mr. Poulose was not getting House Rent Allowance 
given to other agents , and that he was getting Dearness Allowance on the Shastry 
Award scale , and medical benefits which were not given to other agents , and 
that he was also getting salary , which was low and equivalent to the pay given 
to the clerks, I am unable to hold , that all this makes any differnce to the status 
of Mr. Poulose as Agent of the Bank , or to the nature of the duties , that he was 
discharging . The Bank miglit have given him greater facilities in the matter 
of allowing him medical assistance and higher Dearness Allowance . But the test 
in deciding , whether Shri Poulose was a workman or not, is not d pendent upon 
the factors above mentioned . The question will have to be answered by 
reference to the nature of the duties and responsibilities, which lie was dis 
charging , Judged by this test , it cannot be gain said that his duties were mainly 
managerial and administrative. Moreover , it cannot be held , as contended on 
behalf of the workman , that he was exercising only supervisory duties and 
because he was gettlag less then Rs. 500 / - , he must be deemed to be a workman 
within the definition of the term in Section 2 ( s ) . His duties were not merely 
supervisory . They were managerial and administrative, and he was in a position 
of command and control and direction in the branch . It is , however , contended , 
that there is no proof, that Shri Poulose took disciplinary action against any 
employee . There is no force in this argument. It is clear , that he could report 
to the Head Office about disciplinary action to be taken against the staff members, 
working under him , and he was also sending confidential reports about them . 

The fact, that by himself he had no power to appoint or dismiss or remove any 
member of the staff in his Branch does not make any difference . The tact , that 
this could be done only by the Head Office does not sustain the contention of the 
petitioner . Taking therefore , into account, the essential character of the duties 
performed and responsibilities held by Shri Poulose as Agent of Arakunnam 
Branch , it must be held , that they are mainly managerial and administrative 
and not merely supervisory or clerical. Accordingly , I And , that Shri Pouloge is 
not a workman , within the meaning of the Industrial Disputes Act. 


12 . In view of my finding, as above, the Bank was not bound to observe the 
provision of Section 33 ( 2 ) (b ). Therefore , there is no contravention of Section 33 , 
and this petition under Section 33A 18 not maintainable , and this Tribunal has no 
Jurisdiction to entertain the same. I and accordingly on the above issues .

13 . In view of my findings as above, no questions arise regarding the merits 
of the dispute and they cannot be decided herein as this petition is without

10 . From the evidence oral and documentary on record I am satisfied that Indron 
Atmaram was working in the Gokuldoh Mine as ore extractor and that he was a 
direct employee of Shri D . T . Punjabi. Shri D . T . Punjabi has not appeared to 
substantiate his story that the Oriental Mining Corporation was the raising contrac 
tor of the Gokuldoh Mine and that Indroo Atmaram was employed by that Cor 
poration . The contract of service of Indroo Atmaram was thus with Shri D . T . 
Punjabi, as the Proprietor of the Gokuldoh Mine. 

11. It is clear from the notice dated 23rd March 1960 ( exhibit W - 1 ) which has 
been signed by Shri B . A . Bhagat who was the Manager of the Gokuldoh Mine that 
Indroo Atmaram was dismissed for the alleged misconduct of having abused and 
quarrelled with the mates and other officers . It is clear from the evidence that no 
charge- sheet was served upon him for this alleged misconduct nor was any enquiry 
held at which he was given an opportunity to defend himself . The workman has 
denied the misconduct and the matter was taken up in conciliation before the Con 
ciliation Omcer , when Shri D . T . Punjabi did not appear . It is contended on be 
half of Indroo Atmaram that his dismissal was illegal as the rules of natural jus 
tice were not observed and no opportunity was given to him to defend hirnself 
on the alleged misconduct of having abused and quarrelled with mates and Baboos , 
with which he was charged . From the letter dated 21st July 1960 signed on 
behalf of Shri D . T . Punjabi ( Ex . W - 2 ) it is clear that Indroo Atmaram was sum 
marily dismissed for the alleged misconduct of disturbing the peaceful working of 
the Industry without any enquiry . Shri Indroo Atmaram has denied this miscon 
duct and stated that these were false accusations made against him because he was 
an active worker of the union . Raghunath Shivram (WW - 2 ) who was an employee 
of the Gokuldoh Mine at the time Indroo Atmaram was in service and Shri M . 
Deshkar (WW - 3 ) Branch Secrctary of the Samyukta Khadam Mazdoor Sangh have 
given evidence that Indroo Atmaram was an active trade union worker . The Pro 
prietor of the Gokuldoh Mine has not appeared in these proceedings. There is suffi 
cient evidence to suggest that Indroo Atmaram was an active trade union worker 
and his dismissal from service appears to be because of that reason . The question 
18 whether the management of this mine was justjAed in dismissing Indroo Atmaram 
trom service . It is clear from what has been stated above that his dismissal must 
be held to be legal because the rules of natural justice were not observed inar 
much as he was not given any opportunity to defend himself against the miscon 
duct alleged against him . There has thus been clearly a violation of the rules of 
natural justice . The employer has not appeared in these proceedings to justify the 
charges and dismissal. The dismissal must therefore be held to be illegal and un 
justified . There is also no doubt that the Samyukta Khadan Mazdoor Sangh repro 
sents the workmen of the Gokuldoh Mine and that the workmen of the colllery 
through this Union have espoused the cause of Indroo Atmaram and have raised om 
Industrial dispute over his dismissal claiming his reinstatement.

that he had been working on his lands. He cannot, therefore , be deemed to have 
been wholly unemployed during the period since his dismissal on 23rd March 1900 
uptu date . Whilst I am satisfied that an order for reinstatement is justified I do 
not think that the claim of the union for awarding him compensation at the rate 
of his alleged monthly earnings is justified . In my opinion the ends of justico 
would be met if I were to direct that he shall be reinstated in service within 16 
days of the publication of this award with benefit of continuity of service, and that

dismissal till the date of his reinstatement in service .
