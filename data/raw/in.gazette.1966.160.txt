STATE BANK OF PATIALA 

NOTICE 

Patiala, the 1st February 1966 

SBP No. 4 — The following transfers and changes in 
the posting of Bank ’3 Staff are hereby notified : — 

1. Shri Santokh Singh Anand, Officer grade ‘A’, to 
be Chief Accountant of the Bank from the close of 
business on the 12th January 1966 in addition to his 
holding the post of Inspector. 

2. Shri Jaspal Singb, Officer grade ‘C\ held charge 
of Delhi branch, from the close of business on the 18th 
December to the commencement of business on the 
21st December 1965 vice Shri Jagjit Singh Malhotra. 

SBP No. 5 — The following Gazette Notification is 
hereby issued : — 

Redesignation of Officers posted in the Development 
Section at Head Office and important branches (includ- 
ing intensive centres) in connection with the financing 
of Small Scale Industrial Units as ‘Field Officers’ and 
granting them certain powers and responsibilities : — 

Consequent upon the formation of the Development 
Section at Head Office, entxustment of certain discre- 
tionary powers to the Development Officer and the ap- 
pointment of Junior Officers and/or officers grade ‘C as 
‘Field Officers’ at certain important branches for ensur- 
ing that the liberalised scheme for financing the Small 
Scale Industrial Units could be implemented with greater 
care, vigour and success, the Board of Directors of the 
State Bank of Patiala at its meeting held on the 18tb 
June 1965 resolved that :— 

(A) The Junior Officers and/or Officers Grade ‘C’ 
posted for implementing the liberalised scheme 
for financing of Small Scale Industrial Units at 
Head Office and the selected important 
branches be designated as ‘Field Officers’.

(B) The ‘Field Officers’ be entrusted with the fol- 
lowing duties : — 

(a) Survey and re-survey of small scale industrial 
units functioning in the areas of operation of 
the branches where they are posted, and keep- 
ing close and continuous contact with the local 
authorities and associations concerned with the 
development of small scale industries; 

(b) Assisting Branch Manager/ Development Officer 
in processing proposals for advances to small 
scale industries; 

(c) Inspection of stocks, machinery etc. (in 
accordance with the Instructions laid down) 
pledged/ hypothecated to the Bank, as also of 
property, machinery etc. mortgaged to the 
Bank; 

(d) Performing other routine duties, such as, the 
handling of correspondence and compiling 
periodical returns and statements; 

(e) Assisting the borrowers in compiling the finan- 
cial statements; 

(f) Collecting the required information relating to 
market rates of the various items of raw mate- 
rials and finished products held as security 
against the loans and advances, overdrafts 
and other forms of accommodation granted to 
small scale industrialists and also certify the 
valuations stated in the pledge invoices and 
verify, to the extent possible, the value of the 
goods stated in the other documents of title 
to goods such as railway receipts, truck re- 
ceipts etc. received from small scale indus- 
trialists against accommodation granted to 
them; 

(g) Verify the genuineness of the bills and docu- 
ments of title to goods such as railway re- 
ceipts, truck receipts etc. accompanying them, 
as also the clean bills accepted by the Bank 
for granting the aforesaid accommodation; 

(h) Compile and sign and periodically revise and 
sign the opinion reports that are at present 
being compiled and signed by the Head 
Cashiers; 

(i) At a branch which has a ‘Field Officer’ but 

does not have a full fledged Accountant or 
when the Accountant is on leave or his post is 
vacant, the ‘Field OfficeT’ be entrusted with 
the following further duties : — 

(i) Signing delivery orders; 

(ii) Signing drawing powers on accounts; 

(iii) Retaining the godown keys in his cus- 

tody when not in use; 

(iv) Processing applications for medium term 
and instalment credit loans and conduct- 
ing initial and follow up inspections in 
respect thereof, and submitting reports 
regarding such inspections to the Branch 
Manager for transmission to the Head 
Office with his own comments thereon; 

(v) Retaining in his Custody the documents 
relating to the loans and advances and 
other accommodation granted to small 
scale industrialists; 

(Vi) Retaining the bills received for realisa- 
tion and collection drawn on small scale 
industrialists — demand bills being retain- 
ed in joint custody with the Head 
Cashier/ Assistant Cashier; 

(Vii) Exercising full passing power*.

WHEREAS the complainant above-named has made 
a complaint that they have a factory at Madras for the 
manufacture of bleaching clay, that the product of 
bleaching clay manufactured there by them is marketed 
under the name and style of “Bestonite”, that it is used 
mainly in the Vanaspati Industry for bleaching vegetable 
oils and occasionally for bleaching mineral oils also, 
that the raw material for its manufacture is obtained 
from Tandur in the State of Andhra Pradesh, that in 
its manufacture the raw material viz., raw clay is acti- 
vated both thermally and/or chemically by the aid of 
acids, that such a process gives the clay adequate bleach- 
ing properties for decolourising oils; 

That from the time of commencement of the manufac- 
ture of “Bestonite" the complainant has been sending it 
to various centres in India through the Southern Rail- 
way under the head “Spent Bleaching Clay", that the 
Southern Railway accepted the same and charged rates 
as and for "Spent Bleaching Clay”, that the complainant 
was also paying freight as for “Spent Bleaching Clay”, 
that, while so, the respondent demanded payment of 
freight at a higher rate on the basis that the complain- 
ant’s product is “Fuller's Earth”, that from Salt Cotaura, 
Madras, to Ghaziabad it was charged originally at 
Rs. 5.13 per quintal by the Southern Railway, that sub- 
sequently when a further consignment of it was present- 
ed for despatch to Ghaziabad, the railway authorities 
refused to book the same unless freight at Rs. 10.29 per 
quintal was paid, that the complainant paid at the higher 
rate under protest to avoid delay; 

That while the respondent charged (2> of Rs. 4,36 per 
quintal of Bestonite from Salt Cotaurs, Madras, to Indore 
earlier, they insisted payment (2> of Rs. 9.73 per quintal 
later, that the complainant paid the latter charge under 
protest, in order to avoid delay in supplies to their cus- 
tomers; 

That the nature of the complainant’s product, viz., 
“Bestonite” falls only within the description of “Spent 
Bleaching Clay” and not “Fuller’s Earth”, that “Fuller’s 
Earth” is a term used to denote the earth as such, with- 
out any process of activation, that it is mostly an im- 
proved commodity which, unlike the former is used in 
several commercial industries and has various other end 
uses like manufacture of cosmetics, that the cost of the 
raw material necessary for the manufacture of "Besto- 
nite” and the cost of its manufacture are low, that it is 
unreasonable to charge freight for “Bestonite” the rate 
chargeable as and for “Fuller’s Earth”, that the action 
of the respondent in claiming the lre'ght as for “Fuller’s 
Earth" causes unreasonable prejudice to the business of 
the complainant, that the particular description in the 
Goods Tariff as contemplated under Section 28 of the 
Indian Railways Act does correspond to the description 
of “Spent Bleaching Clay”, that the levy of any charge 
on their “Bestonite” as if it were any commodity other 
than “Spent Bleaching Clay" is unreasonable, and has 
prayed for the exercise of the jurisdiction vested with 
the Tribunal to direct the respondent to charge “Besto- 
nite" as blthertofore under the head "Spent Bleaching 
Clay”;
