New Delhi, the 3rd October, 1801. 

S.O. 2448. — In exercise ol the powers conferred by the proviso to article 300 of 
the Constitution, the President hereby makes the following rules regulating the 
method of recruitment of persons to Class III posts in the Indian Agricultural 
Research Institute, New Delhi, namely:- — 

1. Short title.— These rules may be called the Indian Agricultural Research 
Institute (Class III) Recruitment Rules, 1961, 

2. Application. — These rules shall apply to Class III (Ministerial and Non- 
Ministerral) posts in the Indian Agricultural Research Institute, New Delhi, in- 
cluding its Regional offices and sub-stations as specified in column 2 of the Sche- 
dule annexed hereto. 

3. Classification and Scale of Pay. — The classification and the scale ol pay, 

attached to the said posts shall be as specified in columns 3 to 5 of the Schedule 
to these rules. ( 

4. Method of recruitment, age limit and other Qualifications. — The method of 
recruitment to the posts aforesaid, age limit, qualifications, and other matters 
connected therewith shall be as specified in columns 0 to 11 of the Schedule 
al'oresaid. Provided that the upper age limit prescribed for direct recruitment 
may be relaxed in the case of Scheduled Castes/Tribes candidates, displaced 
persons and other special categories in acco: dance with the orders issued from 
time to time by the Government of India. 

5. Disqualification.— (a) No person, who has more than one wife living or 
who having a spouse living, manics in any case in which such marriage is void 
by reason of its taking place during the life-time of such spouse, shall be eligible 
for appointment to service; and 

(b) no woman, whose marriage is void by reasons of the husband having a 
wife living at the time of such marriage or who has married a person who nas 
a wife living at the time of such marriage, shall be eligible for appointment to 
service, provided that the Central Government may, If satisfied that there are 
special grounds for so ordering, exempt any person from the operation of this 
rule.

Agricultural Produce (Grading and Marking! Act, 1937, (1 of 1937), is published, 
as requi ed by the said section for the information of all persons likely to be 
affected thereby and notice is hereby given that the said draft will be taken into 
consideration on or after 15th November, 1961. 

Any objection or suggestion which may be received from any person with 
respect to the said draft before the date specified, will be considered by the 
Central Government. J 

1. Short title. — These rules may be called the Oil cakes Grading and Marking 
Rules, 1961. 

2. Definition. — In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires, — 

(a) ‘Schedule’ means a Schedule annexed to these rules, 

and (b) ‘Agricultural Marketing Adviser’ means the Agricultural Marketing 
Adviser to the Government of India, 

3. Grade designations. — The grade designations to Indicate the quality of oil 
cakes shall be as set out in column 1 of Schedules II to IV. 

4. Definition of quality. — The definition of quality Indicated by the grade 
designations shall be as set out in columns 2 to 9 of Schedules II to IV. 

6. Grade designation marks. — The grade designation mark shall consist cf a 
label bearing the, design set out in Schedule I and specifying the grade desi joba- 
tion. 

6. Method of marking. — (1) The grade designation mark shall be securely 
affixed to each hag in a manner approved by the Agricultural Marketing Adviser-. 
In addition to the grade designation mark, each bag shall be clearly marked with 
the following particulars and in such a maimer as may from time to time bo 
specified by the aforesaid officer: — 

(a) name of packer, 

(b) batch number, 

(c) net weight in kilograms, and 

(d) date of packing. 

(2) An authorised packer may, after obtaining the previous approval of the 
Agricultural Marketing Adviser, mark his private trade mark on the bag in a 
manner approved by the said officer, provided that the private trade mark does 
not represent quality or grade of the oil cake different f r om that indicated by the 
grade designation mark affixed on the package In accordance with these rules. 

7. Method of packing. — Oil cakes shall be packed only in clean and sound jute 
bags. Each bag shall contain 75 kilograms of oil cake of only one type and of 
the same batch, 

8. Special conditions of Certificate of Authorisation. — In addition to condi- 
tions specified in rule 4 of the General Grading and Marking Rules, 1937, the follow- 
ing conditions shall also be the conditions of every Certificate of Authorisation 
issued for the purpose of these rules: — 

(a) An authorised packer shall take all precautions to avoid admixture of 

oil cakes with castor seed and castor coke. 

(b) If an authorised packer handles more than one type of oil cake in the 

same premises, adequate precautions shall be taken by him to avoid 
mixing of different oil cakes. 

(c) An authorised packer shall make such arrangements for testing oil 

cakes as may be prescribed from time to time by the Agricultural 
Marketing Adviser. He shall also maintain proper records of the 
analysis of the samples. 1 

(d) All instructions regarding methods of sampling and analysis, 

packing and marking, and maintenance of records, which may be 
issued from time to time by the Agricultural Marketing Adviser shall

AWARD 

This is a petition under Section 33 -A, of the Industrial Disputes Act. 

2. The petitioner alleges, that the Cochin Commercial Bank has contravened 
the provision of Section 33, of the Act, that the petitioner while working as Agent 
of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank happened to mislay the key of the Cabi- 
net in which the key of the safe was deposited, that after a sho,t while, the 
duplicate key was received, that the activities of the Bank were carried on as 
usual though there was a slight delay, that on 29th December, 1960, the General 
Manager suspended the petitioner, that on 28th February, 1961, the General 
Manager dismissed the petitioner with effect from 17th January, 1901, that the 
petitioner’s appeal to the Board of Directors was unsuccessful, that the order of 
dismissal of the petitioner is illegal, that it should be set aside with suitable 
relief. 


3. The contention on behalf of the Bank is, that the petitioner was an Agent 
of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank, that he was not a workman, that his 
duties were mostly manage, ial or administrative, that accordingly, this petition 
is not maintainable, and there is no contravention of Section 33, that this Labour 
Court has no jurisdiction to adjudicate upon the petition, that on the merits, the 
dismissal of the petitioner is bona fide and lawful, that it cannot be interfered 
with, and that the petitioner should be dismissed, 


4. The issues, that arise for determination are: — 

(1) Whether the petition as brought, is maintainable? 

(ii) Whether there is contravention of Section 33 of the Act? 

(iii) Whether the petitioner is a workman within the meaning of Section 

2(s) of the Act? 

(iv) Whether the dismissal of the petitioner is lawful and justified? 

(v) Whether the dismissal of the petitioner is wrongful and is not bona 

fide? , 

(vi) To what relief, if any, is the petitioner entitled? 


Issues No. 1—3. > 

5 This is a petition under Section 33-A, of the Industrial Disputes Act. The 
petitioner Shri K. M. Poulose was the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch of the 
Cochin Commercial Bank Limited, which has its head office at Cochin, A charge- 
sheet was served against the petitioner, Ext. W/l, dated 29th December, 1960. 
The first charge was, that on 26th December, 1960, as Agent of the Arakunnam 
Branch Mr Poulose mislaid the key of the safe of the Bank, and was unable to 
meet the demands of parties for payment of money, when there was already a 
panic among the public about a rumoured moratorium, and also absented himself 
foThaW an hour from the Office. The second charge was that the petitioner 
kept the key of the safe in the office cabinet at night, which was contrary to 
office orders, The petitioner was directed to furnish his explanation within two 
days Ext M/18, is a report dated 27th December, 1960 that was sent by Ml 
P oulose to the Head Office. It is to the effect, that in his hurry to reach the 
Bank, he could not find out the key of the safe of the cabinet . m Ms] lodge that he 
rushed to the Bank, thinking that the key imght have been kept to the^ Bank 
itself and that it was not found there, and that with the help of Mr, V. J . roinen* 
he managed to get the duplicate key from the Head Office and resumed work. 
Subsequently he traced out the key in his lodge and returned the duplicate key 
to the Head Office. The petitioner begged to be excused for the inconvenience 
caused in this connection. In reply to the charge-sheet Shri Poulose gave the 
explanation Ext. M/19, dated 31st December, 1960, and therein he referred to bis 
report dated 27th December, 1960. He admitted that he had no Idea, that he was

doing a wrong thing, when he kept the key of the safe in the cabinet, and assur- 
ed the Head Office, that he would be more careful in future. Ext W/2, is a 
letter dated 7th January, 1961, by which the management intimated Shri Poulose, 
that the enquiry into the charge framed against him, had been concluded, that 
he was examined on 5th January, 1961, that he was guilty of the charge framed 
against him, that he was guilty of serious mis-eonduct, and that nothing short of 
dismissal would be adequate punishment. The petitioner was asked to show 
cause, why he should not be dismissed from the service of the Bank with effect 
from 17th January, 1961. Ext. W/3. is a letter dated 28th February, 1961, and 
it is stated therein, that the explanation given by Shri Poulose had not convinced 
the management of his innocence, and that he was dismissed from the service of 
the Bank with effect from 17th January. 1961. Ext. M/21, shows, that the peti- 
tioner filed an appeal to the Board of Directors. The appeal was however dis- 
missed as shown by Ext. M/22, dated 25th Ma'ch, 1961. The petitioner’s case is, 
that the order of dismissal passed by the Bank is unlawful, and unjustified and 
that he should be re-instated in service together with back wages. 

6. The preliminary objection has been raised by the Bank, that there is no 
contravention of Section 33 of the Industrial Disputes Act, and that this Labour 
Court has no Jurisdiction to entertain the present petition. However, the conten- 
tion on behalf of the petitione- is, that there is a contravention of Section 33 
because no petition was filed for approval under Section 33(2) (b) of the Indus- 
trial Dispute® Act and one month’s wages were not paid to him as laid down in 
that Section. The management have admitted, that they did not file any applica- 
tion under Section 33(2) (b) for approval, and also did not pay one month’s wages 
as required by that Section, but their answer is, that they were not bound to do 
so, because Mr. Poulose was not a workman within the meaning of Section 2(s) 
of the Industrial Disputes Act, and Section 33 did no apply to him. If the con- 
tention of the management is to be up-held, than it was not necessary for them 
to comply with the conditions laid down in Section 33(2) (b), 

7. Thus the question for determination is, whether the petitioner is a "work- 

man” as defined In Section 2(s) of the Act. It is important to note, that he was 
the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank at the material time. The case 
of the Bank Is, that his duties were mostly managerial and administrative as 
Agent of the Bank. It is well settled, that it is from the nature of the duties 
performed by an individual, that we must judge, whether an employee is a 
workman or not as defined in Section 2(s). It will be necessary to determine, 
whether looking to the nature of the duties performed by Mr. Poulose, it can be 
said, that he was a workman within the meaning of the Section. The General 
Manager of thfe Bank has been examined as MW1. According to him, the peti- 
tioner Mr. Poulose, was an agent of the Arakunnam Branch of the Bank, and not 
merely the Accountant or Accountant Tneharge of the Branch. He was responsi- 
ble for running the Branch, He had a power of attorney (Ext. M/2) in his 

favour, and he was exercising the powers conferred by the power of attorney. 

He was the Controlling Authority in the Branch. He controlled the Staff in that 
Branch. He granted casual leave to them. He was making confidential reports, 
about the Staff. He was making recommendations about increments and promo- 
tions of the Staff unde- him, and his recommendations were usually accepted by 
the Head Office. He could make on the spot decision in an emergency and report 
the matter to the Head Office. Mr. Poulose also deposited cash security as the 
other agents had to deposit. He had the discretion and power to grant over- 
draft®, within the limits of accommodation contained in Ext. M/5. Mr. Poulose 
was accepting gold ornaments and advancing loans to customers after 

determining the limits of such loans. He was checking up the day-to- 

day working of the Branch, He was enforcing discipline of the Branch. The 
correspondence in regard to the Branch with the Head Office, was 

carried on by him. He was accepting gold ornaments and advancing 

loans on them, Shri Poulose was getting Rs. 25/- extra, because he was Agent, 
The Head Office was treating Shri Poulose as an agent and not as a clerk. Mr. 
Poulose was supervising the work of the Branch. He had to allot work to 
members of the staff. He was responsible for the securities and cash of the 
Bank primarily. The securities and money of the Branch, are the responsibility 
of the Agent, though the cashier has also got the key of the safe, Mr, Poulose 
was responsible for the administration of the Arakunnam Branch. He had power 
to take disciplinary action against anv member of the staff. He had pow°r to 

operate accounts in other branches of the Bank. In short the Agent’s dutv was 

to see. that the work of the branch was carried on and he- had power to enforce 
discipline. Thou ch this was not specifically conferred, it was implied in the 
position of Mr. Poulose as Agent.

FI, On a consideration of the evidence on either side, it is established, that 
Mr. Poulose as agent of the Arakunnam Branch, was exercising duties mainly of 
a managerial and administrative character. He was the Administrative Head and 
Manager of the branch. He was in management of the entire affairs of the branch. 
He was the person Incharge of the branch. He was the person responsible to 
the Head Office for the affairs of the branch. He exercised control and super- 
vision over the members of the staff, working under him, and he was even sending 
confidential reports with his recommendations about them about increments and 
promotions He had discretion, initiative and power to grant loans and over- 
drafts to customers of the Bank, though of course within the limits prescribed. He 
had also got the power to operate accounts of other branches of the Bank, and 
that is how he got Rs. 10,000/- from another branch on 20th December 1960, It 
Is not possible to bold, that he was a workman, doing mainly clerical duties. He 
was a person in a position of command and control of the branch at Arakunnam, 
and there was no one above him in that branch. It is important to note, that 
he was able fo take on the spot decisions in an emergency. Thus even in respect 
of the incident, that took place on 26th December I960,. he got into contact with 
the Head Office, and other branches of the Bank, and got cash and the cash was 
received from the Molamthurthi Branch of the Bank. Shri Poulose admits, that 
he had to recommend grant of increments though he adds, that it was in accord- 
ance with the sanction of the Head Office. He was granting casual leave. He 
was sending confidential reports on the forms supplied, He admits, that he sent 
Exts. M/8 to M/13. He also admits, that in the branch, in which, he was working, 
there was no person above him. 


10. From the evidence, it is fairly clear, that Mr. Poulose was exercising duties 
mainly of a managerial and administrative nature. His duties were not manual 
or clerical as attempted to be argued on his behalf. It is, however, urged by 
Mr. T, C. N. Menon, Learned Counsel on behalf of the workman, that he was 
described not as Agent but as Accountant or Accountant-ln-charge. Ext. W/17 
has been relied upon for the purpose of showing, that Shri Poulose was described 
as Aceountant-in-charge, but this is dated 4tb November 1946. Thar further shows, 
that he was appointed Agent with effect from 1st December 194ff of the Peru- 
manoor Office. Ncxtly, attention is drawn to Ext. W/4, dated 19th December 
1963 to show, that Shri Poulose was an Accountant. But this was in the Tri- 
vandrum branch, which was a big branch, and there he was only an Accountant. 
The same remarks apply to Ext. W/5 and Ext, W/0 which show, that Shri 
Poulose was Accountant in the Cochin Branch. Ext. W/8, dated 4th October 
1956, describes him as Agent at Fort Cochin Branch. What is material is, that 
W/10 proves, that Shri Poulose was placed Incharge of the Arakunnam Branch 
from 16th December, 1957, and he was Agent of that Branch since then. The 
contention on behalf of the petitioner that he was only an Accountant or officiat- 
ing Accountant or Accountant-ln-charge, and that this really shows, that he was 
not an Agent, Is not entitled to acceptance. Whatever, he might have beer In 
the past, he was the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch from 1957, and at the 
material time In 1960. It may be, that in the past he acted, as Accountant in 
big offices, like Cochin and Trivandrum, but what is more important and to the 
purpose Is, that Shri Poulose was the Agent of the Arakunnam Branch in Decem- 
ber, 1960, and at the time of dismissal. He was in complete, sole and Independent 
charge of the said Branch, as Agent and was discharging mainly managerial and 
administrative functions in running the business of the Branch. 


. - 11 - It is however, urged that Shri Poulose was only getting the pay. that was 
being paid to clerks in the clerical scale. It is also argued, that he was getting 
medical aid and Exts. W/15 to 16 have been produced in this connection It is 
also clear from the evidence, that he was getting Dearness Allowance, according 
to the scale prescribed in the Shastry Award. It is elicited In the cross-examina- 
tlon of the General Manager, that Agents are given House Rent Allowance, but 
Mr Poulose was not given House Rent Allowance. The Officers not governed 
by the award were getting fixed D.A., but Mr. Poulose was getting D.A.. accord- 
ing to the Shastry Award scale, at the time of dismissal. Officers not governed 



Sec. 3(H)] THE GAZETTE OF INDIA : OCTOBER. 14, 1961 /ASVINA 22, >683 2699 


by the award, did not get medical assistance, but Mr. Poulose was given medical 
benefits. Even granting, that Mr. Poulose was not getting House Rent Allowance 
given to other agents, and that he was getting Dearness Allowance on the Shastry 
Award scale, and medical benefits which were not given to other agents, and 
that he was also getting salary, which was low and equivalent to the pay given 
to the clerks, I am unable to hold, that all this makes any differnce to the status 
of Mr. Poulose as Agent of the Bank, or to the nature of the duties, that he was 
discharging. The Bank might have given him greater facilities in the matter 
of allowing him medical assistance and higher Dearness Allowance, But the test 
in deciding, whether Shri Poulose was a workman or not, is not dependent upon 
the factors above mentioned. The question will have to be answered by 
reference to the nature of the duties and responsibilities, which lie was dis- 
charging. Judged by this test, it cannot be gain said that his duties were mainly 
managerial and administrative. Moreover, it cannot be held, as contended on 
behalf of the workman, that he was exercising only supervisory duties and 
because he was getting less then Rs. 500/-, he must be deemed to be a workman 
within the definition of the term in Section 2(s). His duties were not merely 
supervisory. They were managerial and administrative, and he was in a position 
of command and control and direction in the branch. It is, however, contended, 
that there is no proof, that Shri Poulose took disciplinary action against any 
employee. There Is no force in this argument. It is clear, that he could report 
to the Head Office about disciplinary action to be taken against the staff members, 
working under him, and he was also sending confidential reports about them. 
The fact, that by himself he had no power to appoint or dismiss or remove any 
member of the staff in his Branch does not make any difference. The fact, that 
this could be done only by the Head Office does not sustain the contention of the 
petitioner. Taking therefore, into account, the essential character of the duties 
performed and responsibilities held by Shri Poulose as Agent of Arakunnam 
Branch, it must be held, that they are mainly managerial and administrative 
and not merely supervisory or clerical. Accordingly, I find, that Shri Poulose is 
not a workman, within the meaning of the Industrial Disputes Act. 

12. In view of my finding, as above, the Bank was not bound to observe the 
provision of Section 33(2)(b). Therefore, there is no contravention of Section 33, 
and this petition under Section 33A is not maintainable, and this Tribunal has no 
jurisdiction to entertain the same. I And accordingly on the above Issues.

of the dispute and they cannot be decided herein as this petition is without

2. After the usual notices were issued, a written statement of claim dated 11th 
June, 1961, was received from the General Secretary of the Association of Punjab 
National Bank Employees, Bombay State, Akola, to which the Bank filed its 
written statement in reply dated 22nd June, 1961. Thereafter, Shri B. K. Vyas, 
the workman directly concerned, represented by the Punjab National Bank 
Employees' Union, Vidharbha Region, Headquarters Nagpur, filed another state- 
ment dated 12th July, 1961, to which he annexed a statement containing the list 
of duties performed by him as Appendix ‘A’.

3. At the hearing of the dispute before me on 29th September, 1961, Shri B. 
K. Vyas who was present was represented by the All India Bank Employees’ 
Association and the Punjab National Bank Employees' Union. 

4. It is admitted that Shri B. K. Vyas is an employee of the Punjab National 
Bank in its Khamgaon branch, According to the workman he was appointed as a 
peon on 28th April, 1958, whilst the Bank in its written statement has stated that 
he was appointed as a probationer on 29th July, 1958. In the statement of claim, 
dated 11th June, 1961, it is stated that since Vyas joined service at the Khamgaon 
branch of the Bank, the following duties were taken from him viz., (1) Hundi 
presentation (2) Cash peon (8) Voucher stlt-hing (4) filing of papers (5) Main- 
tenance of old and current record (6) Maintenance of stationery (7) opening of 
dak (8) assisting the despatcher in sorting the dak for purposes of preparing 
envelopes. It is admitted that in addition to his pay as a peon Vyas is being 
paid a special allowance of Rs. 5 per month for performing the duties of cash 
peon. The union’s claim is that Vyas is performing the duties of Daftry and as 
such he is entitled to a special allowance of Rs. 10 prescribed for daftaries by 
paragraph 169 (4) of the Award of the All India Industrial Tribunal (Bank 
Disputes) (hereinafter referred to as the Sastri Award), as modified by section 3 
of th j Industrial Disputes (Banking Companies) Decision Act, 1955 (41 of 1955). 
Before the Conciliator, the Bank denied that Vyas was performing the duties ®f 
a daftary, but stated that he was performing the duties of a cash peon and was. 
therefore, being paid a special allowance of Rs. 5 only as prescribed bv the Sastri 
Award. As no settlement could be reached, the conciliation proceedings ended 
in failure after which this reference was made. 

5. Both parties are agreed that the real question in this reference is whether 
Vyas is performing the duties of a daftary and both parties are agreed that if the 
answer to this question is in the affirmative then Vyas would be entitled to the 
special allowance of Rs, 10 per month under the Sastii Award. In paragraph 
169 of the Sastri Award it is slated that the general observations made in that 
Award in the case of the clerical stall would ordinarily apply to the Subordinate 
Staff also. The Sastry Tribunal in a note below paragraph 164 of its Award under 
the Chapter "Special allowances", had directed for clerks that, "in case where an 
employee comes within more than one category he should be entitled to the 
highest rate of allowance applicable to him.’’ Tt is admitted that this direction 
also applies to the Subordinate Staff. 

0. The union's case is that as Vyas was performing the duties of both cash 
mazdoor and daftary he was entitled to the higher allowance of Rs. 10 per month 
prescribed by the Sastri Award for daftaries. In support of its claim the union 
has relied upon a circular of the Bank, dated 7th March, 1956 (exhibit W-l), which 
is In following terms: — 

“In view of the readjusted emoluments of daftaries in terms of the Award, 
they should be assigned the following duties as laid down in our 
circular No, 40/GM, dated 20th January, 1951. 

1. Filing work and maintenance of flies. 

2. Filing of circulars. 

3. Stitching, binding and numbering of papers and vouchers. 

4. Receiving of stationery from head office. 

5, Arranging of stationery at the tables of the employees. 

In addition to the duties mentioned above, they are expected to do such 
type of work, not of a clerical nature, as is entrusted to i hcm by 
the Manager or Head of the Department. 

Branches are advised to be guided accordingly.” 

7. Shri Mundul, appearing for Vyas stated that all these duties are being per- 
formed by Vyas. as enumerated in the written statements dated 11th June, 1961 
and 12th July, 1961, filed in these proceedings. He has stated that the three duties 
which the management in its written statement denied Shri Vyas was performing, 
viz., (1) maintenance of old and current record, (2) opening of dak and (3) assist- 
ing the despatcher in sorting the dak for the purposes of preparing envelopes, 
are not duties which the daftaries are required to carry out under the bank’s 
circular dated 7th March, 1956. That appears to me to be so. The management 
in its written statement has stated that Vyas is not performing the duties of a 
daftary. It has specifically referred to the thre' duties enumerated above as not 
being performed by Vyas and it is on this basis that the Bank states that he is 
not entitled to the special allowance prescribed for daftaries. Even assuming

that in fact Vy as was not performing these three duties it cannot be held that he 
was not a daftary because those three duties are not duties which daftaries are 
required to perform under the Bank’s head office circular, dated 7th March, 1956 
(exhibit W-l). Vyas in his written statement, dated 11th June, 1961, has stated 
that he was attending to the filing of papers and that duty has not been denied 
by the bank in its written statement, dated 22nd June, 1961, or at a later stage. 
Filing of papers would in my opinion include the first two items of filing work and 
maintenance of files and filing of circulars mentioned in the bank’s circular, dated 
7th March, 1956. It is significant that there is no written denial from the Bank 
of the list of duties which Vyas claims he is performing as listed by him in the 
statement Appendix ‘A’ to his written statement, dated 12th July, 1961. The three 
items of work which the Bank denies Shri Vyas was doing are items Nos. 6, 15 
and 17, but those duties as I have stated earlier are not included in the Bank’s 
Head Office Circular, dated 7th March, 1956 (exhibit W-l), above referred to. 

8. Shri Raghavachary, appearing for the Bank, has referred to the Bank’s 
earlier circular, dated 20th January, 1951 (exhibit E-2), but it is interesting to 
note that the five duties listed in the bank’s circular of 7th March, 1956, were 
included in that circulur as for daftaries of category ‘B’ which would apply to 
daftaries working in small branches. These were admittedly the duties to be 
performed by daftaries category ‘B’ who arc employed in small branches like the 
Khamgaon branch of the Bank. But since the Sastry Award there are no longer 
two categories of daftaries and the daftaries are required to perform the five 
duties mentioned in the Bank’s circular, dated 7th March, 1956. 

9. Shri Raghavachary also sought to rely on the bank’s circular, dated 8th 
September, 1959 (exhibit E-l). This circular only states that enquiries had been 
received from many branches whether work of stitching and binding of vouchers, 
arrangement of stationery on the tables of the employees and placing of stationery 
in the almirahs can be taken from the peons and it was decided that as this type 
of work was essentially of a manual nature and did not require any higher skill 
the branch managers could take such work from the peons. I do not think that 
that circular has any relevancy to the present issue as it is admitted that Vyas is 
also discharging the duties which are normally performed by peons. The claim 
for the special allowance of Rs. 10 has been made because he has discharged the 
other higher duties of daftary listed in the Bank’s circular dated 7th March, 1950. 

10. On the facts as ascertained at the hearing, and on a careful consideration 
of the submissions made by the parties, I am satisfied that B. K. Vyas is perform- 
ing the duties of a daftary and as such is entitled to the special allowance of 
Rs. 10 prescribed under paragraph 169 of the Sastry Award as modified by sec- 
tion 3 of the Industrial Disputes (Banking Companies) Decision Act, 1955 (41 of 
1955). 

11. The next question to consider is the date from which this allowance should 
be paid to him. The union has claimed that this should be paid to him from 18th 
September, 1960, which is the earliest date from which this benefit can be granted 
to him in terms of the Government order of reference herein. This date has 
been specified in the order of reference evidently because the demand was first 
made by the union by its letter, dated 17th September, 1960. Considering that 
the order of reference herein was made on 20tn May, 1961 I think the proper 
date from which to grant Shri Vyas the benefit of the special allowance of Rs. 10 
per month should be 1st June, 1961 and I direct accordingly. I further direct 
that the additional allowance of Rs. 5 per month (Rs. 10 special allowance as 
daftary less special allowance of Rs. 5 as cash peon) from 1st June, 1961, shall be 
paid to Shri B. K. Vyas within 15 days of the date this award becomes enforceable. 

12. The union has applied for costs. Considering that the union has succeeded, 
I think a provision of Rs. 50 as costs in favour of the Punjab National Bank 
Employees^ Union would be justified and I direct accordingly. The amount of 
costs should be paid to the union within a month of the publication of this award 
in the official gazette.
