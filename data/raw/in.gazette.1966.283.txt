Convocation 

The last Annual Convocation of the Institute was held 
at New Delhi on 24th March 1966. The delay in hold- 
ing this year's Convocation occurred due to uncertainties 
prevailing in the country as a result of armed conflict 
between India and Pakistan in September 1965 and the 
subsequent death of the Prime Minister of India, Shri 
Lai Bahadur Shastri. 

The Convocation which took place at the Auditorium 
of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, was 
addressed by Shri D. Sanjivayya, the Union Minister for 
Industry, from whom certificates were received by those 
of the students who came out successful at the Inter- 
mediate and Final Examinations held in January and 
July 1965 and attended the Convocation. The G. Basu 
Foundation Prize of Rs. 300 in cash was awarded to the 
student who was considered to be of outstanding merit 
in 1965. Book prizes were also awarded to students 
who showed merits in Costing.

The Fourth Conference of Asian and Pacific Account- 
ants jointly sponsored by the Institute of Chartered Ac- 
countants of India and this Institute, was held in New 
Delhi, from 29th November to 2nd December 1965. The 
Conference took place m the enchanting auditorium of 
Vigyan Bhavan. T wentyfive accounting bodies represent- 
ing 15 countries participated in the Conference. The 
total number of delegates from India and abroad ex- 
ceeded 1600. 

The Conference nas inaugurated by Hon’ble Shri P. B. 
Gajendragadkar, Chief Justice of India and was addressed 
by Shri A. K, Roy Comptroller and Auditor General of 
India, and Shn S. L. Kulosker, President, Federation of 
Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry. The 
Conference adopted a resolution to hold its next session 
in New Zealand. As a whole, the Conference was a 
grand success.

Cost Audit 

It is now widely known that recent amendment to 
Companies Act has introduced a new Section making 
provision for audit of cost accounts, m case of need, of 
companies engaged in certain specified activities. At the 
Bill stage, the Institute submitted memorandum to the 
Central Government as well as to the Joint Committee 
of Parliament to which the Bill was referred stressing the 
need for conducting cost audit by qualified Cost Ac- 
countants alone and for making cost audit compulsory 
for all companies engaged in production of goods or 
services. A delegation from the Institute also tendered 
oral evidence before the Joint Committee to place Insti- 
tute’s view points more emphatically. 


While many of the Institute’s suggestions were not ac- 
ceptable either to the Joint Committee or to Parliament 
it is a happy augury that cost audit has, for the first time, 
found a place in the statute hook. 'I his is indeed, a step 
forward to wider application ot costing principles and to 
the enlargement of scope of the ptoiession
