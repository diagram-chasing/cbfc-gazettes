A preliminary order was passed in this case on 29-6-1981, which is

" This is a reference under Section 33 C (2) of the Industrial Disputes

The Union has filed a claim statement in which it is contended as 
follows :-The workman was employed as a rubber tapper in the estate

employment appears to have been the result of the workman s demand for 
minimum wages which the employer was compelled to accede to during the 
emergency period . The workman was receiving wages at the rate of 
Rs. 7.80 per day . The union presses for reinstatement of the workman 
with back wages.

reason alleged by the union . The workman was receiving Rs. 7.80 perday 
as wages at the rate of his dismissal. The workman is not entitled to rein 
statement.

entitled to the arrears in back wages at this rate and she will be reinstated

AWARD 
1. This Industrial Dispute between the above parties regarding the 
dismissal of Sri C.P. Krishnan , Store-keeper was referred to this court for 
adjudication as per G.O. (Rt). No.1831/ 78 / L & H dated 30-11-1978 . The 
case was taken on file and notices issued to both parties. They entered 
appearance and filed statements. 

2. The management filed M.P.87/ 1980 to frame a preliminary issue as 
to " whether the dispute referred for adjudication i.e. dismissal of the 
individual worker can be adjudicated before this court as his cause is not 
espoused by the union " . The above issue was heard as a preliminary issue. 
It is not necessary to traverse the entire pleadings to decide the above issue. 
The facts necessary are detailed hereunder : 

3. The wrokman was employed as a Store -keeper under the manage 
mentwhich is a body corporate consisting of 7 primary societies. The 
management dismissed the workman as per the order dated 6-1-1978 . The 
dismissal is stated to be on the basis of the finding that the workman is 
guilty of the charge framed against him in a domestic enquiry conducted for 
that purpose . The validity of the domestic enquiry is challenged by the 
workman . However the same does not arise for consideration at present, 

4. The contention of themanagement is that the management will 
comeunder the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act. Since 
the dispute was raised by the workman himself without the assistance of a 
union or his cause being espoused by a union , the only remedy of the work 
man is to redress his grievances by invoking the provisions of the Kerala 
Shops and Commercial Establishments Act and not under the Industrial 
Disputes Act and as such this court has no jurisdiction to try the dispute . 

5. The contention of the workman is that the management is carrying 
on an industry within the definition on the Industrial Disputes Act and out 
side the purview of Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act and as 
such the dispute is maintainable in this court in view of section 2 A of the 
Industrial Disputes Act . 

6. The dispute between the parties is regarding the dismissal of the 
workman by the management. The dispute will be maintainable in this 
court in view of Section 2 A if the Industrial Dispute Act applies to the facts 
of the case. The contention of the managementas stated earlier is , that since 
the dispute is not espoused by a union , the remedy of the workman is only 
under the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act. The 
Andhra Pradesh High Court had occasioned to consider the above conten 
tion with reference to section 40 and 41 of the Andhra Pradesh Shops Act in 
two full Bench decisions (1) in the case between the Visakhapattanam 
District Marketing Co-operative Society vs. 

Government of Andhra 
Pradesh and others reported in 1977 11 LLJ page 332 and (2)in the case 
between Brindavanam Hotel and Conciliation Officer, Hyderabad and 
another reported in 1978 II LLJ Page 287. In the first case it is held that

“ Even if the Shops Act and the Industrial Disputes Act do not coincide in 
regard to the entire field they cover , there is repugnancy pro - tanto by reason 
of insertion of S.2A in Industrial Disputes Act, whereby individual disputes 
which are not espoused by the union or by other workman , are also brought 
within the ambit of that Act so that a refer :ne can be made under S.10 (1 ) 
of the Industrial Disputes Act in regard to the same . Th : disputes of an 
individual workman , in regard to termination of services, squarely falls 
within the field covered by Sections 40 an ! 41 of the Shops Act and to that 
extent there is repugnancy between the two Acts . In view of the fact that 
the Shops Act has been assented to by the President, the provisions of the 
Shops Act relating to the termination of the services of an employee of a 
shop establishment must prevail over Section 2 A of the Industrial Disputes 
Act and over the other provisions of that Act in far as the 

attracted by reason of the said section 2 A " . In the 
second case cited above, it is held that " If an individual workman 
governed by both these Acts wants to raise an individual dispute , he can do 
so only under the Shops Act, (2 ) If a workmen - union wants to espouse the 
Individual dispute of an individual employee it can do so only under the 
Industrial Disputes Act; and ( 3) In the absence of workmen s union if a 
number of workmen want to espouse an individual dispute of an individual 
workman they can do so only under the Industiial Dispute Act.” 
The provisions of Section 18 of the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establish 
ments Act are similar to sections 40 and 41 of the Andhra Pradesh Shops Act, 
the relevant provisions of which are seen extracted in the above decisions. 
No decision of our High Court expressing a different opinion was brought 
to my notice . Therefore, in view of the decisions cited earlier it has to be 
held that sincethe individual dispute of the workman in this is not espoused 
by the union or by a body of workers, the same can be decided only under 
the provisions of the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act and 
not under Section 2 A of the Industrial Disputes Act. 

7. The contention of the learned counsel for the workman is that the 
management is conducting an industry which is outside the scope of the Kerala 
shops and Commercial Establishments Act and as such the dispute is 
maintainable . The evidence of the workman as WWI is that the manage 
ment is conducting the sale of spare parts , and diesel oil, carrying a workshop 
and conducting an Ice- plant, doing fishing , storing the processed prawn 
and sale of fish . He further deposed that the management is having 61 
mechanised boats which are repaired in the workshop, that spare parts 
are supplied from the store and work in the workshop is carried on with 
electricity and motors. In his cross-examination he admitted that the manage 
ment has been registered under the Kerala Co -operative Societies Act, 
that it gives boats to the members and conduct sale of spare parts . He 
further admitted that there are 7 primary societies under the management 
and that the workshop ismeant for repair of boats lent to members and the 
boats of the outsiders. That the business of the management is more or less 
as stated above, can be seen from the written statement of the management

. 


and Ext.WI the certified copy of the deposition of one Narayanan who was the 
secretary of the management during 1976-77 . Commercial establishment is 
defined in section 2 (4 ) as meaning a commercial or industrial or trading 
or banking or insurance establishment .... " Shop is defined in section 2 (15 ) 
as any premises where any trade or business is carried on or where services 
are rendered to customers....." . The above definition showsthat the business 
carried on by the management comes within the above definition and as 
such it has to be held that the management is a shop or a commercial 
establishment as defined in the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments 
Act . It will no doubt come within the definition of industry as defined in 
section 2 (j) of the Industrial Disputes Act. But the same will not take it 
outside the scope of the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act . 

8. Since the business carried on by the management comes within 
the scope of the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act and since 
the dispute is an individual dispute not espoused by a union or body of 
workers, the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act prevails 
over section 2A of the Industrial Disputes Act, in view of the decisions cited 
earlier and therefore it has to be held that this court has no jurisdiction to 
adjudicate the dispute . The preliminary issue is found accordingly . 

9. In view ofmy above findings, I pass the award holding that 
this court has no jurisdiction to adjudicate the dispute . 

10. This award will come into force 30 days after the date of publica 
tion in the Official Gazette .

II. (1) There will be a Judging Committee (hereinafter called the 
Committee) to examine and select the best films, artistes and technicians 
who are to be given awards. 

(2 ) " The Committee will consist of seven members, including the 
Chairman. The term of the non -official members of the Committee will be 
three months from the date of constitution of the Committee. The Director 
of Public Relations or his nominee from the Public Relations Department 
will represent the Government in the Committee and will function as the 
member Secretary of the Committee. The other six non -official members 
including the Chairman , to be nominated by the Government, will be 
persons who are distinguished in the field of motion picture , art, culture, 
literature or aesthetics" . 

(3 ) If the Chairman of the Judging Committee finds it inconvenient 
to attend two or more meetings of the committce , the committee will have 
the power to elect a working Chairman. 

(4 ) The quorum of the Committee shall be more than half the 
number of members. 

(5 ) No person who has a share in the production or distribution of 
any film entered for the awards shall be a member of the Committee. 

(6 ) The awards will be decided by the Government on the 
recommendation of the Judging Committee . The Committee will make 
their recommendations to Government. 


(7 ) The decisions of the Government of Kerala will be final in 
respect of the Awards and of interpretation of these Rules and no appeal 
shall lie against them . 

(8 ) A person who participates in the Kerala State Awards for 
Malayalam Films under these rules shall be deemed to have accepted these 
rules.

Explanatory Noto 
( This note is not a part of the notification , but is intended to indicate 
its general purport). 

The number ofmembers in the Kerala Public Service Commission 
was enhanced from four to six in the year 1971. The quantum of work in the 
P.S.C.has since increased considerably and therefore Governmenthave deci 
ded to enhance the number of members from Six to Eight to cope up with 
the increased work load and to amend the Kerala Public Service Commission 
(Composition and Conditions of Service ofMembers and Staff ) Regulations, 
1957 accordingly . 

The above notification seeks to achieve the above object.

Esplanatory Note

The Kerala Public Works Department Electrical Wing Executive 
Employees Association has requested Government ( 1) to promote the 
Diploma holders also as Electrical Engineers/Executive Engineers (Elec 
trical) and to reserve 5 % of the posts to Diploma holders and (2) to 
delete the provision of making appointment to the post of Electrical 
Engineer by promotion of Assistant Executive Engineer (Radio ). 

As per the existing rules, Diploma holders are not eligible for 
promotion as Electrical Engineer. In the Electrical branch there is 
only one post of Electrical Engineer . The Chief Engineer , General, 
Buildings and Roads is therefore of the vicw that since there are a 
large number of Electrical Graduates and there is only one post of 
Electrical Engineer, preference should be given to graduates. He has 
therefore recommended that graduates in Electrical Engineering or its 
equivalent with a minimum service of 3 years as Assistant Executive 
Engineer (Electrical) can be promoted as Electrical Engineer and that 
in the absence of such qualified hands, Diploma holders with 5 years 
service as Assistant Executive Engineer ( Electrical) can be promoted . 

On examining the question it has been found that the Chief 
Engineer has proposed to promote Diploma holder Assistant Executive 
Engineer (Radio ) to the category of Executive Engineer (Radio ). As there is 
therefore no justification for denying the same benefit to the Diploma

In G , O. (P ) No. 91/74/Fin . dated the 5th April, 1974 (pay revision 
orders) Government have interalia ordered that after 1-4-1974 , incre 
ments will be granted from the first day of the month in which it 
falls due. These orders are applicable to aided school staff also . In 
the case of Government employees, the provision in the pay revision 
orders referred to above has been incorporated in the Kerala Service 
Rules Part I, as Government decisions 1 and 2 under rule 31. But so 
far as aided school staff are concerned , such provisions have not been 
made in the Kerala Education Rules and hence they lack in statutory 
backing. It is proposed to amend the rule to give statutory validity 
to the above orders. In rule 7 of Chapter XXIV ( B ) of Kerala 
Education Rules, there is no reference to increment . This may give 
room for doubt whether the provisions in rule 61 Chapter XIV (A ) 
Kerala Education Rules regarding increment applicable to aided school 
teachers are mutatis mutandis applicable to non -teaching staff of aided 
schools. It is proposed to amend the rules to make the provision 
clear on this score. The amendment is intended to achieve the above 
objoct.
