and Associates. They are entitled to use the letters 
“F.I.C.W.A.” and “A.I.C.W.A.”, respectively, after their

tically according to their names.

the Membership Number of that member. “F” after a 
member’s name denotes that he is a Fellow, and “A” 
after a member’s name denotes that he is an Associate. 
The name of the place and the Region to which he be- 
longs have also been indicated. 

An asterisk (*) before a member’s name indicates 
that the member is in practice.

Registration of Students 

During the year under review 7840 students have been 
enrolled. This was the record annual registration in the 
history of the Institute. The total number of Registered 
Students, excluding those who have discontinued their 
studentship, comes to over 26,068. 

Examinations 

The Intermediate, Final and Management Account- 
ancy Examinations were held as usual in July 1964 and 
January 1965. A summary of results of the above 
examinations is given in the statement attached. 

The examinations were held at the regular centres at 
Ahmedabad, Bangalore, Bombay, Calcutta, Delhi, 
Gauhati, Hyderabad, Jabalpur, Jaipur, Jamshedpur, 
Lucknow, Madras, Nangal, Patna, Poona, Trivandrum 
and Waltair. In addition, four new examination centres 
were also opened at Cuttack, Durgapur, Nagpur and 
Ranchi during the year under review. 

Compulsory Coaching 

The compulsory Coaching Scheme initiated by the 
Council has come into operation from 1st January 1965. 
Under the Scheme, at persons registering their names as 
students on or after that date shall be required to under- 
go a course of tuition for a prescribed period before they 
become eligible to take the examinations of the Institute. 
The form of tuition is either postal conducted exclusive- 
ly by the Institute or oral conducted by any other insti- 
tution recognised by the Institute for the purpose. The 


tuition is also available, at their option, to students who 
registered their names before the dato of commence- 
ment of the Scheme. Till the end of March 1965, over 
300 students, including those who voluntarily joined the 
Scheme, were enrolled under the Postal Scheme. 

It is hoped that the Scheme will ere long begin to 
show its effect on examination results.

Companies Amendment Bill 

The Council records its appreciation of the action 
taken by (he Central Government in introducing in Par- 
liament, the Companies (Second Amendment) Bill, 
1964 contemplating in one of its proposed amendments 
to acquire power to order audit of cost accounts, when 
considered necessary, of companies engaged in certain 
specified activities. The Council has communicated to 
the Central Government its own views so far as cost 
audit is concerned. Besides submitting memoranda to 
the Central Government and the Joint Committee of 
Parliament to which the Bill was referred, oral evidence 
on behalf of the Council was also tendered before the 
Joint Committee. The Joint Committee has already sub- 
mitted its report to Parliament incorporating its consider- 
ed views. The Bill is now awaiting discussion in Parlia- 
ment, and much will depend upon the form in which it 
will be passed, The Council hopes that the passage of 
the Bill through Parliament will open up a new vista to 
the Costing profession in India,
