By Order No . 6 / 36 /65 -LRII dated 30th August 1965 , the Central Govern 
ment referred for adjudication an industrial dispute between the employers in 
relation to the Toposi Colliery , Dt. Burdwan , and their workmen in respect of 
the subject matter mentioned in the following scheme: 
" Whether the dismissal of Shri Raju Mahato, Pick Miner of Pits 8 and 9 

of Toposi Colliery , with effect from the 25th November 1964 was an 
act of victimisation by the management ? If so , to what relief is the 

workman entitled ? " 
2 . Raju Mahato was a pick miner employed at Pits 8 and 9 of Topoși colliery . 
According to the management on 5th November 1964 during the first shift , Raju 
Mahato was detected cutting a pillar towards the beginning of the shift and 
the Overman asked him not to do such an illegal act but to cut coal from the 
working face allotted to him . At about 3 P . M . he was again found cutting the 
pillar and thereupon he was reported against and a chargesbeet dated 6th 
November 1964 was served on him . Raju Mahato gave a reply on 7th Novem 
ber 1964 wherein he denied the charge of cutting any pillar and stated that he 
was always cooperating with the management and the officers, the latter state 
ment being in reply to the charge of wilful insubordination . The manage 
ment did not consider the reply satisfactory and fixed 13th November 1964 at 
8 - 30 A , M . for an enquiry by Shri K . Dey , Asstt. Manager and Shri D . N . Kar , 
Labour Welfare Officer. An enquiry was held accordingly by the above men 
tioned ofcers who submitted their report on 20th November 1964 Anding that 
the charges were proved and recommending dismissal. The manager for 
warded the report to the Director for his approval, and after obtaining approval 
from the Director , he passed an order of dismissal on 25th November 1964 . 
Raju Mahato represented the matter to Shri Robin Chatterjee, Vice - President 
of Colliery Mazdoor Sabha of which he was a member . Robin Chatterjee sent 
a complaint to the Conciliation Officer ( C ) , Raniganj on 1st December 1964 
about the dismissal of Raju Mahato alleging that the dismissal was wrongful 
and was victimisation for the trade union activities of Raju Mahato . The effort 
at conciliation however failed and so the matter is now before the Tribunal. 

3 . The case of the union is that Raju Mahato was a leading member of the 
union , the Colliery Mazdoor Sabha at Toposi colliery and he joined other pick 
miners in filing petitions to the Manager and to the Labour Inspector and other 
Government authorities about the grievances of the pick miners of Topogi 
colliery , e . g ., existence of black stone. mixed up with the coal in Pits 8 and 9 
and reduction of wages of pick miners on account of deduction for the stone 
chips found mixed up in the coal ; non - supply of free picks by the manage 
ment to the pick miners ; non payment for lead and empty pushing and so on ; 
and that the management was, therefore displeased with him and other lead 
ing union workers, and that in persuance of a policy of getting rid of such 
workers , the management served a false chargesheet on the workman and dis 
missed him and this constituted victimisation . The union further alleged that 
the domestic enquiry was not held properly in accordance with the principles 
of natural justice. 

4 . Ext. 6 is a letter from Raju Mahato and 3 other pick miners addressed 
to the Manager, Toposi colliery , complaining about the non - supply of measure 
ment slips mentioning the lead and empty pushing. This complaint is however 
dated 7th November 1964, i. e ., when the chargesheet dated 6th November 1964 
had already been served on Raju Mahato and therefore the complaint has little

Accordingly the management was asked , after the arguments had been com

6 . Apart from the statement made by Anil Bowri. there is the evidence of

Mahato was seen cutting a pillar and that when N . N . Goswami found him 
nçutting the pillar at about 3 PM , of 5th November 1984 and protested , Raju 
Mahato adopted a deflant attitude. Apart from the evidence of Anil Bowri, the 
evidence of these 3 witnesses is sufficient for the finding of the Enquiring Om 
cers that the charges had been proved against Raju Mahato . 

7 . Raju Mahato himself stated at the enquiry that one tub was found over 
turned and its contents scattered on the tram line, and that he asked the in 
charge babu to allow him to load half tub from the scattered coal as he had

there was some quarrel between the incharge babu or Munshi babu and Raju 
Mahato after that. The Enquiring Officers considered the defence evidence 
and rejected the defence version and accepted the version of management s 
witnesses. A tribunal does not sit in appeal over the finding of the Enquiring 
Officers provided there is some evidence to support the finding. Accordingly , 
though Raju Mahato repeated before the Tribunal that the Munshi babu had 
asked for Re. 1 / - from him and threatened that he would complain if he did 
not give Re, 1 / - to the Munshi babu , and denied having cut the pillar , that is 
no reason for holding that the domestic enquiry was not properly conducted . 
"But it may be pointed out that this story about the munghi babu or incharge 
babu having asked for Re. / - 1 as bribe did not occur in the reply . Ext. 2 , which 
was given by Raju Mahato to the chargesheet nor does it occur in the written

statement was necessarily filed long after the enquiry , it is clear that the
