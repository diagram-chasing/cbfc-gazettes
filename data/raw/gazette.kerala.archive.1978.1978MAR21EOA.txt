union . A statement signed by these 10 workers to the effect that they had 
resigned from the former union and joined the petitioner s union has also 
been appended to the impleading petition . Though no final order is seen 
to have been passed on the petition it has to be taken that the petition was 
allowed as the claim statement filed by that new union (the 3rd union above ) 
has been accepted and enquiry continued as if that union was impleaded .

The 3rd union has filed a claim statement concerning its 10 workers 
(workers 4 to 12 and 22) and contending as follows. All the workmen 
covered by the reference were attached to the Kottayam Office/Workshop 
of M /s Kulathungal Corporation , why were the agents for Tata Diesel 
Vehicles in Kerala. On 12-4-1971 the 1st employer put up a notice saying 
that the agency of Tata vehicles had been terminated from 31-3-1971 and 
consequently the Business was being closed down and services of workers 
were terminated . But the officers had stopped attending office by that 
time and there was no one to settle the claimsof the workmen or even to 
pay their wages for March 1971 . Therefore the workmen continued to 
squat in the workshop premises from 12-4-1971 . On 20-4-1971 the Tahsildar 
att ched the movables in the workshop for arrears of revenue due from 
Kulathungal Corporation . The 2nd employer put forward a claim before 
the Tahsildar that they had taken over the agency of Tata Vehicles from 
January 1971 and that they had taken lease of the workshop premises and 
purchased the movables in the workshop from the 1st employer on 
20-1-1971 . Though the claim was rejected by the Tahsildar, it was upheld 
in appeal by the Revenue Board . The Tahsildar then handed over 
possession of the movables and premises to the 2nd employer on 30-1-1971 . 
Thus the 2nd employer is the successor-in -interest of the 1st. The denial 
of employment to the workers is illegal and unjustified as the 2nd employer 
had taken over the entire business. The 2nd employer was bound to take 
over the workmen also . So the workmen have to be treated as having 
continued in service notwithstanding the change in management. 
They are also entitled to wages from 1-3-1971 and 

they had 
actually worked till 12-4-1971. Since their nonemployment after that date is 
illegal and unjustified they are entitled to get wages for the subsequent 
period also . Hence an award has to be passed setting aside the orders of 
termination of service and directing the management to treat the workmen 
as having continued in service without break and to pay full back wages. 

The Kottayam District Motor Workers Union Congress (the lst union ) 
has filed a statement adopting the above contentions of the 3rd union . The 
2nd union has not filed any statement. 

The 1st and 2nd employer have filed separate written statements. 

The 1st employer s contentions in brief are these: - M /s Tata Engi 
ncering and Locomotive Co. Ltd., Bombay gave notice of termination to 
the 1st employer in December, 1970 terminating their agency with effect 
from 1-4-1971 and the 2nd employer was selected by the manufacturers as 
their agents. Government attached the movables on 20-4-1971 for sales tax 
arrears due from the 1st employer, but the attachment was subsequently 
released by the Board of Revenu in favour of M /s Motor and General 
Finance Ltd., New Delhi. In such circamstances the 1st employer had no 
option, but to close down the business. In fact after getting notice of 
termination of agency in 1970 there was practically no work going on in 
the workshop . The fact of termination of agency , closure of business and 
termination of services of the workmen were intimated to the workmen 
and the 1st employer has paid all compensation and wages due to the 


4 


employees as per law . So they are not entitled to any relief from the 1st 
employer . It is not correct to say that notice was issued to the workers 
only on 12-4-1971. By an agreement executed between the 1st employer 
and M /s Motor and General Finance they obtained possession of the 
property on 22-1-1971 and the movables were sold to them on 20-1-1971 
for Rs. 5 lakhs. Out of the 10 workmen (workers 4 to 12 & 22) represe: ted 
by the 3rd union , 6 workers (workers 4 , 7 and 9 to 12) and out of the 
3 workers (workers 1 to 3 ) represented by the 1st union 2 workers (workers 
1 and 2 ) have also settled their dispute with the 1st employer after filing of 
the claim statement. There were altogether 52 employees in the Kottayam 
Office. Out of which only 5 are thus now pursuing the dispute, while 
others have settled their dispute with the 1st employer admitting 
the closure as bona fide. Hence the claim can never be an industrial 
dispute and the impleading petition is also not maintainable. The claim 
has therefore to be dismissed as against the 1st employer. 

Over and above the above contentions of the 1st employer the 2nd 
employer contends as follows. He is an employee of M /s Motor and 
General Finance Ltd., 17. B. Asfali Road, New Delhi (hereinafter 
called the company) . The company has nothing to do with the 1st employer 
and so he is an unnecessary party to the proceedings. Since the company has 
not been impleaded the reference and the claim statement are unsustainable. 
At the time of the attachment by the Tahsildar , the workshop and the 
movables were in the possession and enjoyment of the company and they 
were finally released to the company pursuant to the decision of the Board 
of Revenue. It was the company which was appointed as agents from 
29-4-1971 terminating the agency of M /s Kulathungal Motor Corporation 
from 1-4-1971. The movables were purchased for Rs. 5 lakhs on 20-1-1971 
and the workshop premises were taken on lease on 21-1-1971 by the company. 
The company is not carrying on business as a successor in interest of the 
Ist employer . This is evident from the fact that out of the 7 districts over 
which the 1st employer had agency only in 5 districts the company was 
appointed as agents. The workersmentioned in the order of reference are 
not workers of the company and so they are not entitled to get any employ 
ment in the company . At any rate the workers are not entitled to get the 
arrears of pay from the company. 

The 3rd union has filed a rejoinder traversing the contentions of the 2 
employers, reiterating the union s claim and further stating that Mr. Umesh 
Sanan is the legally constituted representative of the Motor and General 
Finance Ltd. which has taken over the business of the Kottayam Branch 
from January 1971 and the denial of employment of the workers occurred 
in April 1971. 

The short points that arise for consideration are: 
(1) Are the workmen entitled to re-employment with continuity of 

service ? 
Are they entitled to wages from 1-3-1971? 
Reliefs and Costs? 


5 


The evidence consists of Exts . W1 to W27, MI to M8 and CI to C3 
and also the testimonies of WWI to WW4 and MWI to MW5. 


Point 1:-- According to the union, the 2nd employer stood in the shoes 
of the 1st employer and is the successor-in -interest of the Ist employer. But 
the facts do not support this. The Ist employer was the agent for Tata 
Diesel Vehicles and the workmen concerned were working under the 1st 
employer in the Kottayam Office a d workshop . And now , no doubt the 
2nd employer has that agency and the office and workshop . That by itself 
is not sufficient to hold that the 2nd employer is the successor- in - interest of 
the 1st employer. MW3 is the Director and Sales Manager of Tata Engi 
neering Locomotive Co., and he has proved Ext. ČI to C3. It is an 
admitted fact that it was that company which granted agency to the 1st 
employer. Ext. Cl is copy of notice dited 30-12-1970 issued byMW3 to 
the 1st employer terminating their agency with effect from 31-3-1971. 
Ext. C2 is copy of letter dated 29-4-1971 sent by MW13 proposing to accept 
M /s Motor and General Finance Ltd., New Delhi as their agents. And 
Ext . C3 is copy of the formal agreement executed pursuant to Ext. C2 on 
19-9-1972 . The 2nd employer asMW4 has admitted that he was the repre 
sentative of M /s. Motor and Gneral Finance Ltd., for Kerala . The records 
amply prove that the agency of the 1st employer was terminated with effect 
from 31-3-1971 . For taking over the agency, the 2nd employer had made 
necessary preparations. They purchased the workshop tools, machines and 
service egipments for Rs. 5 lakhs on 20-1-1971 as per Ext. M7 dated 
20-1-1971 and took a lease of the premises from the 1st employer as per 
Ext. M4 dated 22-1-1971 , though Ext. M4 allows the employer to occupy the 
premises till 22-3-1971. These facts especially the termination of the agency 
of the Ist employer and granting of fresh agency to the 2nd employer would 
only show that the 2nd employer was not a successor- in - interest of the 1st 
employer. There was no transfer of management as contempl..ted in 
Section 25 FFF of the Industrial Disputes Act. The further fact that many 
of the employees had received compensation from the 1st employer admitt 
ing closure as genuine supports the above conclusion only. Ext. W27 was 

brought to my notice by the counsel of th : union . That is a letter by the 
2. 2nd employer to one of its employees on 26-1-1971 for assistance to get 

vehicles ec. with the help of the Ist employer. That cannot affect the 
conclusion to which I have arrived at. There is no other record or evidence 
also to displ :ce my earlier conclusion . That it is a case of closure of the 
undertaking is proved by circumstance also . It is the case of the union in 
the claim statement that on 12 4-1971 the 1st employer put up Ext. MI 
notice to the effect that their agency was terminated by 31-3-1971 and 
consequently services of the workers were terminated and the business closed 
down and the officers had stopped attending office by that time so that there 
was no one to settle the claimsof the workmen and even to pay their wages 
for March 1971. When there was such a closure the 2nd employer is not 
bound to reinstate or pay compensation . The workmen were entitled only 
to compensation as provided under Section 25 FFF of the Industrial Disputes 
Act, ie . notice and compensation in accordance with the provisions of 


6 


Section 25F as if the workmen have been retrenched , and that too from the 
Ist employer, and not for reinstatement with or without back wages. I 
find this point accordingly. 


Point 2 : It is the case of the union that the workmen have actually 
worked till 12-4-1971 as notice of closure was given to them only on that 
date . On the otherhand the managements case is that the workmen were 
given notice of closure much early. Ext.Mi is admittedly that notice of 
closure on 12-4-1971. The initial with the date 12-4-1971 on its margin de 
notes that it was approved only on that date and hence the notice was put 
on the notice board only on 12-4-1971, and not before. WWIand WW2 
are workers No. and 5 respectively in the order of reference . WW4 was 
the accountant of the 1st employer. All these witnesses swear that they had 
worked till the evening of 12-4-1971 and Ext Ml notice was published only 
then . But MW1 and MW2, two other workers swear that the notice was 
published in March 1971 itself. But I feel their evidence is uni cliable 
because they are interested in the management; as admitted by than they 
were ab orbed subsequently in the service of the 2nd employer and they got 
compensation also from the 1st employer for termination of service . Still, 
MW2 indirectly admits there was work there even after Ext. Mi. So the 
workmen were entitled to wages from 1-3-1971 to 12-4-1971 . 


Point 3:-I have already held that the workmen are not entitled to re 
employment and that they are entitled only to nosice and compensation in 
accordance with Section 25F as if they have been retrenched and also to 
wages from 1-3-1971 to 12-4-1971. 

Though the order of reference is in respect of 22 workmen , the claim 
statements of unions 1 and 3 limit it to 13 persons, 9 having received 
compensation from the 1st employer and gone away in peace. According to 
the written statement of the 1st employer excepting 15 workmen (workers 2, 
5 , 6 , 8 and 22 ) the others have also settled their dispute with the 1st 
cmployer. This is not specifically denied in the replication. WW4 admits 
th t excepting 3 or 4 persons others have already received their compensa 
tion . Exts . M5 andM6 receipts according to MW4 are receipts given by 
workers 4 ant 12 in the order of reference for having received compensa 
tion . But there is no data to fix the compensation due to workers 2 , 5, 6 , 
8 and 22 Wages and other details of the workmen are also not available . 
In these circumstances I think fair and reasonable to pass an award declar 
ing the rights of the above suid 5 workmen and leaving them to seek their 
remedies under Section 33 C (2 ) of the Industrial Disputes Act. 

In the result, I hereby pasz an award declaring the rights of the 
workmen Nos. 2 , 5 , 6 , 8 and 22, who have not received their due compen 
sation , to notice and compensation from the 1st employer and in accordance 
with Section 257 of the Industrial Disputes Act, as if they have been retre 
nched and also to wages from 1-3-1971 to 12-4-1971. 


7 


They may if so advised work out their remedies under Section 33 C (2) 
of the Industrial Disputes Act. Parties will suffer costs in the circumsta 


nces.

The case ofthe union briefly stated is this: 

The worker Sri Shihabudeen was the Chief Salesman in the vegetable

his explanation denying the charge. On 26-12-1969 he was given another 
memo saying that an enquiry will be conducted against him on 27-12-1969. 
The disciplinary committee found him guilty and terminated his services 
from 24-1-1970. The termination of service is illegal as the worker was 
denied natural justice. In the memo of charge dated 8-7-1969 the worker 
was not even called upon to submit his explanation . There was clubbing 
of different charges before the so -called enquiry and that was illegal. Before

and he was made to affix his signature. None of the witnesses 
was examined in his presence. Inspite of written request by te

to the worker. The management did not produce the enquiry proceedings 
before the conciliation officer, though promised to produce the same. There 
was no impartial observer or any worker of the establishment to watch the 
proceedings. The finding of guilty by the committee is perverse and wrong . 
Since the accused worker was an active worker of the union , there was victi 
misation .

Point 1:-A contention as to jurisdiction has been raised by the manage 
ment based on Section 69 of the Kerala Co-operative Societies Act. But that 
contention cannot stand in view of the ruling reported in 1973 K.L.T. 523 
which says that in such matters the Registrar has no jurisdiction , but only 
the Labour Court has.. Another contention as to jurisdiction raised is on 
the ground that the Kerala State Co -operative Tribunal is to decide the 
dispute . But this contention was not pressed before me. Hence I find that 
this court has jurisdiction . 

Point 2 : The domestic enquiry is assailed by the worker as improper 
and as an act of victimisation on several grounds as already stated in detail. 
One of the grounds is questions and answers of witnesses examined in the 
enquiry were recorded by the disciplinary committee according to its whims 
and fancies. I think that ground must sustain for the following reason . 
Ext. E3 contains the depositions of the witnesses examined . It is stated in the 
written statement that 4 witnesses were examined by the management and 
cross-examined by the worker with the help of his union representative. But 
Ext. E3 discloses that only 2 witnesses were examined by the management 
and out of the 2 witnesses only one was cross-examined . Further it fails to 
disclose that the cross -examination was conducted by the worker with the aid 
of any one else. Then again when the management has taken the stand in 
their written statement that 4 witnesses were examined for the management 
and the enquiry proceedings disclose that only 2 witnesses were examined , 
it is for the management to explain how the depositions of the other 2 
witnesses are missing or how the discripancy has occurred . 
EW1who is the President of the Consumer Stores and who was the President 
of the Enquiry Committee and who alone was examined before this Court for 
the management has not given any explanation . The result is when 4 
witnesses were examined for the management at the domestic enquiry and 
the depositions of 2 of them have been suppressed the enquiry smacks 
ofsome foul play and supports the case of the union that there was 
victimisation . Moreover the worker as WW1 has sworn that all 
questions and answerswere not correctly recorded and that the answeres

manipulated to the disadvantage of the worker. 

Perusing the records of the case it would appear that the case of the 
management is this. On 8-7-1969 when the Secretary of the Consumer s 
Store inspected the vegetable stall, of which the claimant was the Chief Sales 
man , he found the salesman selling vegetables at a higher price than that 
fixed in the price fixation register for his gain . On 3-10-1969 the Secretary 
inspected the stall again . Then he found that the salesman had 
purchased 40 old cucumbers from his brother s shop and 20 out of them have 
completely decayed and the others have partially decayed . He further 
noticed that the salesman had purchased Savala and Potatoes from local 
market against instructions of the manager. I may say here that the 
Secretary has not been examined before Court or in the domestic enquiry. 

On the 1st date of inspection , ie . 8-7-1969 itself Ext. W3 order of 
suspension was issued by the Secretary to the worker alleging irregularities 
in the sale of vegetables. No explanation was called for in Ext. W3. The 
union sent Ext. W4 protesting the suspension order and demanding

be conducted against him the next day ie. 27-10-1969. There was no 
specific charge given to him before as to what matters the enquiry would be. 
Ext. W11 was given only the previous day of the proposed enquiry. The 
above facts be speak the truth that sufficient opportunity was not given to 
the worker to prepare to face an enquiry and canons of natural justice were 
not at all adhered to by the management. 

Though the enquiry committee consisted of 3 members, it appears from 
Ext. E3 minutes that the enquiry was conducted by the President alone . 
In the above circumstances I unhesitatingly find that the domestic enquiry 
conducted in this case was not proper. 

Now I come to the issue referred to this Court for adjudication and 
that is reinstatement ofthe worker with backwages. In considering that 
issue I have to consider first whether the charge against the worker is 
proved . From what I have stated under point I, it is clear that the 
evidence recorded in the domestic enquiry cannot be relied on . Before this 
Court also sufficient evidence has not been let in by the management to 
warrant a finding of guilty against the worker . Ext. Él is the price fixation 


register for 16-5-1967 to 24-12-1969 and Ext. E2 is the duplicate cash bills 
relating to that period . A comparison of the two would disclose that sale of 
vegetables was not always made in tune with the prices fixed in the register. 
But it is in evidence that there wasmore than one salesman in the vegetable 
stall and so the blame cannot be place solely on this worker. It has not 
been proved through an expert that the handwriting any particular bill 
is thatof this dismissed worker . It is surprising to note that the manage 
ment has not stated anywhere how much loss the management sustainrd 
a ::d how much this worker gained by selling against the prices indicated in 
Ext. El. Thus there is no legal evidence to hold that the worker is guilty 
of any charge; the finding of the enquiry officer is such that a reasonable 
person would not have arrived at on the materials before him and hence is 
perverse. I find accordingly . 

The enquiry was vitiated and the finding of the enquiry officer was 
perverse and therefore this court will be justified in interfering with the 
decision ofthe management dismissing the worker by quashing the same. 
Consequently the management will have to be directed to reinstate the 
worker , but regarding back wages we have to consider some aspects. The 
worker was dismissed on 24-1-1970 , that is about 8 years back . To say that 
he was all along idling his time sounds unreasonable. He was to find out 
some work and thus minimise the liability of the ma iagement. He cannot 
lie idle all these years and ask themanagement to comperisate. So I think 
a direction to pay back wages for 12 months would be reasonable. The 
worker as WWI has stated that his monthly salary was Rs. 160 and that has 
not been challenged in cross-examination . 

In the result , I pass an award quashing the dismissal order passed by 
the management and direct them to reinstate the worker Sri A. Shihabudeen 
with continuity of service and back wages for twelve months at Rs. 160 p.m. 
Parties are to suffer costs in the circumstance .

As desired by both sides, they were heard on the preliminary issue

It was first argued on behalf of the union that the Enquiry Officer was 
interested in the management and has therefore held the charges 
against Sri Mathai even though there were discrepancies in the testimonies 
of the witnesses for the management and the witnesses did not swear to the 
start of the alleged incident, the Union s case being that Sri M. P. Joseph

turned round and beat Joseph with his tiffin-carrier. The enquiry officer 
was examined asMW2on commission and the enquiry proceedings were 
marked through him . So also the Manager of themill who was present at 
the time of the enquiry was examined as MW1. But nothing was brought 
out to discredit their evidence which is in full support of a normal and fair 
domestic enquiry. It is in their cvidence that though the enquiry, officer

of the firm that Nattakom Rubber Mills is managed by a separatemanager

there is no sufficient reason for the management or the enquiry officer to be

workmen were given notice showing the name of the pers & appointed as 
the enquiry officer and they participated in the domestic enquiry without 
raising any objection to the Enquiry Officer. To state that the enquiry 


officer was interested after completion of the enquiry I think is only an after

worker was issued a charge sheet detailing the charges against him . He was 
given the list of witnesses. The charge was read out to him and he was 
asked whether he had committed the offence. Witnesses were called one 
by,one and examined and cross-examined in the presence of the accused

The above is the preliminary order passed by me on 8th September, 
1977. I have extracted above the entire preliminary order for the sake of 
easy understanding of the facts of the case and the evidence adduced in the 
case . As has been held in 1975 (2 ) L. L. J. 379 Supreme Court when a 
case of dismissal or discharge of an employee is referred for adjudication , 
the Labour Court must first decide, as a preliminary issue, whether the 
domestic enquiry has violated the principles of natural justice . If the 
domestic enquiry is found improper or defective, then the management will 
have to be given a chance to adduce fresh evidence before this court for the 
purpose of proving the misconduct alleged against the workmen . In such

PART I 
LEGAL AID AND ADVICE 


1. Short title and commencement.- (1) These rules may be called the 
Kerala Legal Aid and Advice to the Poor and Conduct of their Cases Rules, 
1978 . 

(2 ) They shall come into force at once . 
2. Definitions. In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires: 

( a ) " Public Counsel" n.eans a Government Pleader appointed by 
Government under these rules. 

(b ) “ Case" means a suit, application , petition or complaint institu 
1ed or filed , as the case may be, and includes in respect of civil proceedings 
a suit, application or petition proposed to be instituted or filed as the case 

(c ) Court" means a Munsiff s Court or a Magistrate s Court, or a 
District Court or a Sub Court in a Taluk Headquarter . 

(d ) “ Legal Aid ” means the aid given by the Government under 
these rules to a person by engaging a Public Counsel for the conduct of his 
case in a court or tribunal and includes any legal advice given to a person 
in respect of a case under the rules by a Public Counsel.

may be.

(e) “ Family” for the purpose of these rules shall include husband , 
wife, unmarried children , father and mother. 

(f) " Poor Person " means a person , who is a resident of the Kerala 
State and whose average monthly income from all sources does not exceed 
Rs. 300 . 

Note: Income of members ofthe family of the applicant shall also 

be taken into account to decide whether a person is poor or 

not. 
(g ) " Scheduled Castes" , and " Scheduled Tribes” mean the Sch . 
Castes and Sch . Tribes specified by the Constitution ( Sch . Castes) Order, 
1950 and the Constitution (Sch . Tribes) Order, 1950 respectively and 
pertaining to the State of Kerala, including the converts from such Sch . 
Castes and Sch . Tribes. 

(h ) " Tribunal" means any tribunal constituted by Government 
under the provisions of the Kerala Land Reforms Act, 1964 or the provisions 
under Labour Enactments. 

3. Scope and extent of the legal aid. --The legalaid under these rules shall 
be given to all cases and proceedings before any Munsiff s Court, Magistrate 
Court, Sub - Court, District Court, Land Tribunal or Labour Tribunals in 
The State. 

4. Persons eligible for legal aid and advice.- (1 ) The following persons 
shall be eligible for legal aid and advice: 

(a ) Any poor person as defined in clause (f) of rule 2 . 

(b ) A member of a S.C. or S.T., whose average monthly income 
does not exceed Rs. 300 

(c) A person who is allowed to sue or appeal informa pauperis .

the Tahsidar of the Taluk in which the Court or tribunal is situated where

sworn to and filed by the applicant regarding his monthly income.

sanctioning legal aid , enquire or cause an enquiry to be made into the 
application and after satisfying himself that the applicant is eligible for 
legal aid under the Rules, allow the application and direct the concerned 
Public Counsel to appear in and conduct the case or proceedings, on behalf 
of the applicant or to give legal advice in a case referred to by him 

(2 ) The Tahsildar shall accept the affidavit sworn to and filed by 
the applicant regarding his monthly income on its face value. 

(3) Application for legal aid shall be disposed of within fifteen days 
from the date of its receipt under intimation to the applicant.

11. Withdrawal of legal aid – (1) Legal aid granted to any person in 
any case may be withdrawn by the Tahsildar at any time on any of the 
following grounds. 

(a ) his incomehas since increased resulting in the cessation of his

( b ) hehas engaged a lawyer other than the Public Counsel assigned 
to him ;

(d ) there is lack of deligence on his part in the conduct of the case.

In exercise of the powers conferred by Art. 229 of the Constitution of 
India, the Chief Justice hereby makes the following amendmentto the Kerala 
High Court Service Rules , 1970, namely, 

AMENDMENT 

(CS.28 . ) 
In the said Rules, 
the words and other rules" occurring in rule 35 shall be deleted . 

( By order) 

K.B.MENON ,

Explanatory Note 
( This is not part of the amendment but is intended to indicate its 
general purport.) 

In James Thomas ard others v . Chief Justice, High Court of Kerala 
and others ( 1977 K.L T.622 (FB) ] it was held that the words other rules" 
occurring in Rule 35 of the High . Curt Service Rules will attract the 
provisions of the Kerala State and Subordinate Services Rules to the High 
Court Service. As the High Court Service Rules are meant to be Self 
Contained Rules and the required provisions of the Kerala State and 
Subordinate Services Rules and Kerala Civil Services (Classificati in 
Control and Appeal) Rules have , where nece- sary been adopted by the 
High Court Service Rules, it is considered unnecessary to allow these Rules 
to be swept in by a residuary clause . Hence the amendment.

THE KERALA STATE HOUSING BOARD ESTABLISHMENT 

REGULATIONS, 1977 


In exercise of the powers conferred by Sub - section (1) of Section 162 of the 
Kerala State Housing Board Act, 1971 (19 of 1971), the Kerala State Housing 
Board hereby makes the following regulations, the same having been approved 
and confirmed by the Government in G.O. (Ms) 267 /77 /LĂ & SWD..dated " 
17-9-1977 as required by Sub -section (3 ) of the said section . 

REGULATIONS 
1. Short title.- (1) These regulations may be called the Kerala State 
Housing Board Establishment Regulations, 1977 . 
2. Definitions. In these regulations, unless the context otherwise requires , 
(a ) Board means the Kerala State Housing Board , constituted under 

section 4 of the Kerala State Housing Board Act, 1971 (19 of 1971). 
(b ) Employee means an officer or servant of the Board . 
(c ) General Rules means the Kerala State and Subordinate Service 

Rules, 1958 as amended from time to time. 
(d ) Public Service Commission means the Kerala Public Service 

Commission. 
(e) Secretary means the Secretary of the Board . 
3. Qualification and methods of recruitment.- (1) No person shall be eligible 
for appointment to the cațegories in col. (1) by the methods specified in 
column (2) of the table in Annexure-A unless he possesses the qualifications 
specified in the corresponding entry in column (3) thereof. 

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub -regulation (1), a person 
holding any particular category of post on a regular basis on the date of 
coming into force of these regulations shall be exempted from possessing the 
G. 24/MC 


qualifications, prescribed for that category of post under these regulations . 
But promotion of such a person to any higher category shall be considered 
only if he possesses the qualifications prescribed for the higher post under 
-these regulations. 

(3) In these regulations, wherever possession of experience is prescribed 
as a qualification for appointment to any category in addition to a general 
or academic or technical qualification , only the experience secured after 
acquiring the said general or academic or technical qualification shall be 
" reckoned . 

4. Qualification relating to age . -Save as otherwise expressly provided in 
Annexure-Ă , no person shall be eligible for appointment by direct recruit 
ment to any of the posts under the board if he has not completed or will not 
complete 18 years of age or has completed or will complete 30 years of age on 
the first day of January of the year in which applications for appointments 
are invited with the usual relaxation in the case of backward classes, sche 
duled castes , scheduled tribes and Ex-servicemen as is allowable for direct 
recruitment to Government service from time to time. 

Provided that age relaxation upto 40 years shall be allowed to the re 
gular employees of the Board who apply to the Public Service Commis 
sion for direct recruitment to any category if they possess the other quali 

fications prescribed under these regulations for that category . 
5. Probation .-Every person appointed to any of the categories shall from 
the date on which he joins duty, be on probation. 
(i) if recruited direct or by transfer for a total period of two years 

on duty within a continuous period of three years and 
( ii) if appointed by promotion, for a total period of one year on duty 

within a continuous period of two years.. 
6. Declaration of selection posts. - Promotion to the following posts shall be 
made from select lists prepared from among eligible officers on the basis of 
merit and ability, seniority being considered only where merit and ability 
are approximately equal. Persons included in the select list shall be ranked 
in the order of their seniority . 

(a) Assistant Secretary 
(b ) Accounts Officer 
(c) Senior Superintendent 
(d ) Regional Engineer 
(e) Assistant Engineer/Personal Assistant to Housing Board Engineer 
(C) Assistant Architect

7. Preparation of select lists by Departmental Promotion Committee.- ( 1) The 
select lists of persons eligible for appointment to the posts mentioned 
in regulation 6 above shall be prepared in accordance with the pro 
cedure laid down in General rule 28 (b ) of the Kerala State and Subordinate 
Services Rules, 1958 by a Promotion Committee constituted for the purpose. 

(2) The Promotion Committee for the purpose of sub-regulation (1) shall 
consist of 

(a ) a member of the Public Service Commission who shall be the 
Chairman of the Committee . 

(b ) a nominee of a Government not below the rank of Deputy Secre 
tary to Government from the Department in charge of Housing. 

" The Secretary of the Housing Board . 
(3 ) The Promotion Committee may associate a technical officer of the 
Board when found necessary.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS 
Section 14 of the Kerala Panchayats Act, 1960, section 57 of the Kerala 
Municipalities Act, 1960 and sections 46 and 47 of the Kerala Municipal 
Corporations Act, 1961, provide for the adoption of the relevant portions 
of the electoral rolls of the Legislative Assembly Constituency for purposes 
of the elections to the panchayats, municipalities and municipal corporations 
respectively . The electoral rolls of the Legislative Assembly Constituencies 
are those prepared under the Representation of the Peoples Act, 1950, and 
under that Act only persons who are not less than twenty -one years of age are 
entitled to be registered in the electoral roll. 

2. Government have decided that the voting age for the elections to 
the local bodies in the State be reduced to eighteen years . For implementing 
the decision , the electoral rolls for the panchayat areas, municipalities and 
cities have to be prepared seperately and necessary provisions for the prepara 
tion and revision of the rolls periodically, independent of the electoral rolls 
for the Legislative Assembly Constituencies , have to be included in the Kerala 
Panchayats Act, 1960 , the Kerala Municipalities Act, 1960 and the Kerala 
Municipal Corporation Act, 1961. 

3. The Bill seeks to achieve the above object . 


FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM 
The Bill , if enacted and brought into operation , would not involve any 
expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of the State .
