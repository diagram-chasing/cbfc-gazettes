RESOLUTION 

New Delhi , the 19 th June 1968 

No, 6/1/68 -I&EC , — -The Tariff Revision Committee was 
originally set up under the late Ministry of International 
Trade Resolution No. 10/8/63 GATT, dated the 17th March, 
1964, with Shri S, Subramanian as Chairman, in order to 
recommend the lines oil which lhe Indian Customs Tariff 
might be revised and improved and to examine certain allied 
matters, Subsequently, the Committee was also asked, vide 
Ministry of Commerce Resolution No. 1 (54)-TRC/66, dated 
lhe 1st February. 1966, to examine the classification of 
Commodities In the Import Trade Control Schedule and 
make recommendations for its revision. From the 1st Sep- 
tember, 1967, Shri P. D. Kasbekar, Chief Controller of 
Imports & Exports, became the Chairman. . The Committee 
submitted its report on the Import Trade Control Schedule 
on die 28th February, 1968. 



514 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA, JUNE 29, 1^68 (ASADHA 8, 1890) [Part I— Sec. 


2. As regards the main question referred to it, namely the 
revision of the Import Trade Control Schedule, the Com- 
mittee has pointed out that the Government have already 
agreed in principle that the Import Customs Tariff should 
be revised and based on the Brussels Tariff Nomenclature. 
The Committee has come to the conclusion that once the 
Customs Tariff is revised, the revision of the Import Trade 
Control schedule will become inevitable. It has recom- 
mended that the revised Import Trade Control Schedule be 
based on the revised Customs Tariff at the level of the main 
headings. The Committee has also made several recom- 
mendations on matters germane lo the objective of its 
enquiry, such as the format and contents of the Red Book, 
the weekly meetings between the Customs and the Imp on 
Trade Control Organisation, the procedure for dealing with 
Import Trade Control enquiries, functions of the Import 
Trade Control Headquarters Coordination Committee, and 
the procedure for dealing with appeals to the Customs Autho- 
rities involving the Import Trade Control Regulations. It 
has devoted particular attention to the question how the 
enunciation of Import Trade Control Policy and the detailed 
writing out of import licences can be improved so as to 
minimise delay and difficulties at the time of customs 
clearance. 


3. Government arc in general agreement with the Com- 
mittee’s recommendations. The introduction of a revised 
Import Trade Control Schedules, will have to wait the revi- 
sion of the Customs Tariff Schedule, which is in its final 
stages. As regards the various procedural suggestions, some 
have already been given effect to and others will be imple- 
mented to the extent and as soon as possible. 

4. Government wish to place on record their appreciation 
of the comprehensive enquiry carried out by the Committee 
and the several useful recommendations which it has made.. 


ORDER 

Ordered that a copy of the Resolution be communicated 
to all concerned. 

Ordered also that the Resolution, be published in the 
Gazette of India for general information, 

S, RAM ACH AN D ARAN, Jt. Secy.

No, 20(6 )/68-M///. — As a result of the recommendation 
of the Mineral Policy Conference held in January, 1947, 
regarding the urgent need for formulation and pursuance of 
a National Mineral Policy and for central coordination and 
planned development of the country’s mineral resources and 
the need for establishment of central machinery for the pur- 
pose ? the Indian Bureau of Mines was set up in March, 

1948, to function primarily as an advisory body. 

2. With the enactment of the Mines and Minerals (Regu- 
lation & Development) Act by the Central Legislature, in 
September 1948, and the Notification of the Mineral Con- 
cession Rules, 1949, and the Petroleum Concession Rules, 

1949, the Bureau's sphere of work increased considerably. 
Apart from its purely advisory capacity, it was found neces- 
sary for the Bureau to function as a field and research orga- 
nisation also, to attend to the conservation of the country’s 
mineral resources and their utilisation in the best possible 
manner. The functions of the Bureau as set out earlier 
were, therefore, expanded to suit the above requirements in 
1953 and 19551 

3. On the other hand, the Geological Survey of India, 
which was set up by the Government of India in 1851, was 
responsible for geological mapping and exploratory drilling 
calculated to delineate mineralised zones in the country. In 
1953, the latter functions were transferred to the Indian 
Bureau of Mines. In 1955 it was, however, clarified that 
the Bureau would conduct drilling and other prospecting 
operations to prove and estimate the workable reserves in 
mineral deposits only in areas indicated by the Central 
Government, Tn course of time, some overlapping of func- 
tions between these two organisations had crept in and to 
eliminate avoidable duplication, the. prospecting, drilling and 
mining divisions of the Indian Bureau of Mines were merged 
with the Geological Survey of India with effect from 
M-1966. 

4. The Indian Bureau of Mines was left with three main 
divisions for carrying out the residuary functions, viz, : 

(i) Mineral Economics Division. 

(ii) Ore Dressing and Pilot Plant Lab oratories; 

(iii) Mines Control and Conservation of Minerals 
Division. 

5. There was a feeling that the above set up of the Indian 
Bureau of Mines was not adequate for achieving the objec- 
tives of conservation of minerals and the development of 
mineral wealth of the country consistent with the increasing 
requirements oE industry and export trade. The mining in- 
dustry is iu need of technical assistance and consultancy for 
scientific development of mines and installation of modern 
mining equipment and introduction of modern mining tech- 
niques and practices. The existence of sizable deposits of 
low grade ores also nosed important problems of beneficia- 
tion and economic utilisation. 

6. A Committee to review the organisation of the Indian 
Bureau of Mines and its functions in the context of these re- 
quirements was, therefore, set up on 22-7-1966. 

7. Inter- alia, the Committee was asked to suggest how the 
Indian Bureau of Mines can be reorganised, equipped and 
strengthened to provide systematic guidance, advice and con- 
sultancy to the mining industry, and in particular, the 
following services 

(a) appraisal of mineral deposits;

(b) design of mines, including selection of mining 
techniques; 

(c) bcneficiation, ore dressing, concentration and extrac- 
tion of metals; 

(cl) design and selection of suitable mining equipment 
and the preparation of How-sheets and diagrams. 

8, The above Committee, after considering the various 
aspects of the functions Off the Indian Bureau of Mines made 
a number of recommendations, most of which have been

9, The recommendations of the Committee in .respect of

and the mining industry* 

In so far ns, the retransfer of the Prospecting & Explora- 
tion Wing from the Geological Survey of India to the Indian 
Bureau of Mines is concerned, it is felt that the prospecting 
and exploration work should continue to remain as part of 
the Geological Survey as was the arrangement prior to 1953. 
This would ensure effective coordination between the survey

ensuing work relating to detailed prospecting and exploration 
on the other, This arrangement will also avoid any hiatus 
in the exploration work and at the same time cut out possible 
duplication. Moreover, in recent years, the trend has been 
for the exploiting agencies in the public and the private 
sectors themselves to take up detailed prospecting work prior 
to exploitation. It is, therefore, now unnecessary for a third 
agency like the Bureau to step in. 

10. There arc many spheres, particularly in respect of 
development of mines, their conservation, bcneficiation and 
research pertaining to the mining industry and particularly 
the statutory functions vested in the Bureau under the 
Mineral Conservation & Development Rules, 1958, for ins- 
pection of mines in which the Bureau must function as a 
separate entity. Thus, should the Bureau be merged with 
the Geological Survey of India, it would not be able to per- 
form these functions effectively, 

On the other hand* the Geological Survey of India, which 
is a ‘research oriented organisation with specific functions such 
*as geological mapping, prospecting and exploration leading 
to the discovery of mineral deposits, cannot be assigned the 
statutory functions of control and conservation of mines and 
minerals which must be shouldered by an independent 
organisation like the Bureau of Mines. 

Moreover, in recent years, the Geological Survey of India 
has grown into a vast organisation and any attempt to com- 
pletely merge the Indian Bureau of Mines with the Geologi- 
cal Survey of India would, therefore, tend to make the 
organisation unwieldy. While such an arrangement is bound 
io affect the scientific, prospecting and exploration work of 
the Survey on the one hand, responsibilities with regard to 
mineral conservation and development of mines might at the 
same time suffer* The Bureau, of necessity, has therefore to 
function as an independent entity as it was conceived 1 at the 
very beginning. 

To enable the Indian Bureau of Mines to undertake the 
additional responsibilities of research and of rendering advice 
to the mining industry, both in public and private sectors of 
the mining industry, as well as mining departments of the 
State Governments, it has been decided to add consultancy 
as one of ils functions. 

11. In view of the above, the functions of the Bureau 
shall be ; — 

(i) Providing Technical Consultancy’ services on pay- 
ment of fair and reasonable charges to the 
mining industry, both in the private and public 
sectors, in regard to appraisal and exploitation of 


mineral deposits, planning of designs of mines, 
selection and design of suitable mining equipment, 
research and preparation of economic flow sheets 
for beneftciation and concentration of minerals; 

(ii) Well-pJanned selected inspection of mines for effect- 
ing systematic development of mineral deposits, 
elimination of avoidable waste, promotion of im- 
proved methods of mining and reduction of mining 
costs; 

(iii) Conducting research in special mining problems in 
collaboration with Central Mining Research Station 
and other research institutions; 

tiv) Conducting research on beneficialion of low grade 
ores and determinative separation of complex ores 
and the industrial utilisation of minerals and mineral 
products in collaboration with National Metallurgi- 
cal Laboratory and other research organisations; 

* v) Conducting analyses of ores and minerals in con- 
nection with the work of the Bureau, and also for 
the mining industry (in both private and public 
sectors) on payment of fair charges; 

<vi) Collection and publication of statistic* relating to 
mineral production in India, mineral stocks, exports, 
local consumption, elc. 3 and collection and mainte- 
nance of information regarding world mineral pro- 
duction, world mineral trade, foreign mining rules 
and other related matters; 

(vii) Publication of bulletins and monographs on inves- 
tigations relating to mining and mineral industry; 
preparation of mineral maps leading to a complete 
inventory of mineral resources of India; and 

(viii) Advising the Central and State Governments on 
any matter connected with the mineral industry, 
particularly on the exploitation and utilisation of the 
country’s mineral reserves.
