on the 11th October, 1973 is published for general infor-

THE NORTH-EASTERN AREAS REORGANISATION 

(REMOVAL OF DIFFICULTIES) ORDER NO. H. 

In exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section (1) of 
Section 87 of the North Eastern Areas (Re-organisation) 
Act, 1971 (81 of 1971), and all other powers hereunto 
enabling, I, V. V. Giri, President of Tndia, hereby make the 
following Order, namely — 


1. (1) This Order may be called the North Eastern Areas 
(Re-organisation) Removal of Difficulties Order No. II. 

(2) It shall come into force at once. 


2. Where, by virtue of the provisions of the North Easte 

Areas (Re-orgamsution) Act, 1971 (81 of 1971) the Sta 
or Union Territory in which the registered office of a cor 
pany is situate on the 21st day of January, 1972, bears 

name different from that stated in the memorandum 
association of that company, then, notwithstanding anythil 
contained in the Companies Act, 1956 (1 of 1956) the sa 
memorandum shall, as from that day, be deemed to ha 
been altered by the substitution of the name of the State 1 

Union Territory, as the case may be, in which it is situa 


on that day and accordingly ihe Registrar of Companies, 
having jurisdiction in the State or Union Territory concer- 
ned, shall make necessary alterations in memorandum of 
association of the said company.

S.O. 3099.— In exercise of the powers conferred by section 
17 of the Export (Quality Control and Inspection) Act, 
1963 (22 of 1963), the Central Government hereby makes 
Ihc following rules, namely : — 

1. Short title and commencement. — (1) These rules may 
be called the Export of Cumin Seeds (Inspection) Rules, 
1973. 

(2) They shall come into force on the 3rd December, 
1973. 

2. Definition. — In these rules, "cumin seed” means the 
seed obtained from Cnmlnum Cymlnum L, whole or pow- 
dered, produced in India. 

3. Procedure of Inspection of Cumin seed prior to 
export.- — 

(1) The provisions of the Agricultural Produce (Grad- 

ing and Marking) Act, 1937 (1 of 1937), the 
General Grading and Marking Rules, 1937, and 
the Cumin Seeds Grading and Marking Rules, 
1969, shall, so far as may be, apply to the ins- 
pection of cumin seeds prior to export. 

(2) Tf on inspection of the consignment of such cumin 

seeds, the Agricultuial Marketing Adviser to the 
Government of India, Directorate of Marketing and 
Inspection or any other officer of that Directorate, 
authorised by him in this behalf, is satisfied that 
the same complies with the standard specifications 
and has beep labelled and packed according to 
these rules, he shall issue a Certificate of Grading 
in taken of its export-worthiness.

S.O. 3102. — Whereas, for the development of the export 
trade of India, certain proposals for subjecting cumin seeds 
to quality control and inspection prior to export, were pub- 
lished, as required by sub-rule (2) of rule 11 of the Export 
(Quality Control and Inspection) Rules, 1964, in the Gazette 
Or India, Part II — Section 3 — Sub-section (ii), dated the 15th 
luly, 1972, under the order of the Government of India 
in the Ministry of 1'oreign Trade, No. S.O. 1704, dated the 
15th luly 1972 ; 

And whereas objections and suggestions were invited till 
the 14th of August, 1972, from all persons likely to be 
affected thereby ; 

And whereas the said Gazette was made available to the 
public on the 15th July, 1972; 

And whereas the objections and suggestions received from 
the public on the said draft have been considered by the 
Central Government ; 

Now, therefore, in exercise of the powers conferred by 
section 6 of the Export (Quality Control and Inspection) 
ACtj 1963 (22 of 1963), the Central Government, after con- 
sulting the Export Inspection Council, being of the opinion 
that it is necessary and expedient so to do for the develop- 
ment of the export trade of India, hereby— 

(1) notifies that cumin seeds shall be subject to quality 
control and inspection prior to export ; 

(2) specifics the type of inspection in accordance with the 
Export of Cumin Seeds (Inspection) Rules, 1973, as the 
type of inspection which shall be applied to such cumin 
seeds prior to export ; 

(3) recognises the grade designations formulated under the 
Cumin Seeds Grading and Marking Rules, 1969, as the 
standard specifications for cumin seeds ; 

(4) prohibits the export in the course of international 
trade of cumin seeds unless a mark or seal or label 
recognised by the Central Government has been affixed 
or applied to packages or containers of such cumin seeds 
and is accompanied by a Certificate of Grading issued by 
the Agricultural Marketing Adviser to the Government of 
India, Directorate of Marketing and Inspection, or any other 
officer of that Directorate, authorised by him in this behalf, 
in token of its export-worthiness. 

2. Nothing in this order shall apply to export by sea, 
land or air of samples of cumin seeds not exceeding In 
value of rupees twenty to the prospective buyers. 

3. In this Order “cumin seed" means the seed obtained 

from Cmninuni Cynilmmi L, whole or powdered, produced 
in India. 

4. This Order shall come into force on 3rd December, 
1973.

AWARD 

Tho Central Government in the Ministry of Labour, Em- 
ployment & Rehabilitation (Department of Labour & Employ- 
ment) being of the opinion that an industrial dispute exists 
between the employers in relation to the management of

pect of the mattcis specified in the schedule of reference, by

schedule

Bhowra (Dhanbad), was justified in stopping Shri 
Nath Mahto, Pick Miner, from work with effect 
from 1st July, 1968? If not, to what relief is the 
workman entitled?’’ 

2. The reference was received in this Tribunal on 27-1-70 
when it was registered as reference No. 8 of 1970. Before 
1 proceed further I like to mention here certain facts 
admitted by the workmen as well as by the employers in 
their petitions filed before (he Tribunal in the present pro- 
ceeding and also the submission made by the representatives 
of all the parties at the time of argument. The reference 
as it stands gives an impression that M/s. Karamchand Thapar 
& Bros (P) Limited weie the owneis of Bhowra Colliery 
in question, which is not a fact according to the case of all 
the parties. The fact is that Bhowia Colliery belonged to 
Bhowra KanKaoce Collieries Limited and M/s. Karamchand 
Thapar & Bros (P) Ltd., weie the Managing Agents of the 
said Company with icspect to Bhowra Colliery and some 
other collieries. By operation of Law the managing agency 
was abolished with effect from 1-1-70. Therefore, from 
1-1-70 M/s. Karamchand Thapar & Bros (P) Limited ceased 
to exist as Managing Agents with respect to the colliery in 
question. The industrial dispute in question was sponsored 
by Colliery Mazdooi bangh by raising the dispute before 
the Asstt Labour Commissioner (Cential), Dhanbad as per 
their petition dated 13th/16th July ’68 alleging wrongful 
stoppage of the concerned workman from work with effect 
from 1-7-68, At that time M/s. Karamchand Thapar & 
Bros (P) Limited were Inchnrgc of the management of the 
colliery in question as Managing Agents thereof. As pointed 
out above, the reference was made on 17-1-70 and at that 
time M/s. Karamchand Thapar & Bros (P) Limited ceased 
to be the Managing Agents of the colliery and accordingly 
on that date Ihe owners of the colliery, namely, M/s. Bhowra 
KanKancc Collieries Limited were Tnchargc of the manage- 
ment and control of the colliery. M/s. Karamchand Thapar 
& Bros (P) Ltd., did not file any written statement in this 
case but Bhowra KanKanee Collieries Limited as employers 
filed written statement on 17-11-70, It is the admitted case 
of all the parties that by a scheme of amalgamation approved 
by the Hon’ble High Court at Calcutta in Company Petition 
No, 123 of 1970 connected with Company Application No. 412 
of 1969 M/s, Bhowia KanKanee Collieries Limited were 
amalgamated with M/s. Oriental Coal Co. Limited. Thus, 
in view of the said amalgamation. M/s. Bhowia KanKanee 
Collieries l imited ccused to exist in their independent right 
and M/s. Oriental Coal Co. Limited became the owners and 
Incharge of (he management of Bhowra Collierv. According- 
ly, us per Order No, 19 dated 20-9-72 M/s. Oriental Coal 
Co, Ltd., was impleaded as a party to the present reference. On 
that very date M/s, Oriental Coal Co. Limited tiled a petition 
adopting the written statement already filed bv Bhowra Kan- 
Kanec Collieries Limited Thereafter, durine the pendency of 
the piesent proceeding the collieiv in question vested in the 
Cential Government under Coking Coal Mines (Nationali- 
sation) Act, 1972 (Act No 36 of 1972) and under the

4. The admitted facts which arise from the written state- 
ments of the employers and the workmen aic these. The 
concerned workman Shri Nath Mahato was a permanent 
employee in Bhowra Colliery and he was working as pick 
miner in that colliery. As he became sick he was sent 
to Central Government Hospital at Dhanbad for treatment. 
He was admitted in that hospital on 1 1-5-68 and he re- 
mained as indoor patient there from 1 1 -5-68 to 30-6-68 
whereupon he was discharged from the hospital. The Medi- 
cal Officer of Central Gqyt- Hospital, at the time of dis- 
charge, recommended for light job on surface and the 
workman reported for duty on 1-7-68 with the said medical 
certificate. 

5. The case of the workmen is that the management re- 
fused to provide him light surface duty or to allow him to 
resume his original job as pick miner and thus the concerned 
workman became out of employment. The case of the 
woikmen was then taken up by Colliery Mazdoor Sangh 
before the Asstt. T.abour Commissioner (Central). Dhanbad

in stopping the workman Shri Nath Mahato from work is 
unjustified and accordingly their prayer is for a direction

wages and other allowances from the date of his stoppage 
from work i.c. from 1-7-68. According to the case of the 
management the workman became unfit to carry out or to 
perform his duties as a pick miner, the job in which he 
was appointed. There was no li^ht job on surface in 
which the workman could be appointed available at that 
time and that the management was not bound to provide 
the workman with light surface iob to which ho was not 
appointed. It is further alleged that neither the concerned 
workman nor the union, who took up his cause, wanted 
the workman to work in his original job i.c, underground 
pick miner. The case of the management accordingly is 
that the reference should be decided against the workman. 

6. On behalf of the management the only witness exa- 
mined is MW-1 Sri R B, Singh, the Personnel Officer in 
Bhowra Colliery. On behalf of the workmen 2 witnesses 
have been examined and they are Sri Kedar Nath Singh 
(WW-i) and Sri Nath Mahato (WW-2), the concerned work- 
man. WW-1 has been working in Bhowra Colliery from 
1946 and he is at present working in that colliery as Over-

wrilten statement on behalf of the workmen was also filed

W-l to W-3, have been exhibited. 1 shall refer to the oral 


and documentary evidence adduced by the parties in course

7 . Ext. M-4 is the letter of the Medical Officer of the 
colliery to the Superintendent of Central Hospital, Dhanbad 
sending the concerned workman for treatment. The letter 
is dated 11-5-68. Admittedly the woikmnn remained an in- 
door patient in the Central Hospital, Dhanbad from 11-5-68 
to 30-6-68 wheieupon he was dischaigcd with a medical certi- 
ficate granted by the Medical Officer of that hospital re- 
commending surface duty for the workman. It appears that 
the workman produced the certificate before the Medical 
Officer of the colliery who granted fitness certificate dated 
1-7-68 (Ext. W-2) mentioning therein that the woikman 
Shri Nath Mahato was fit to resume duty for surface duty 
as recommended by Central Hospital. According to the 
evidence of MW-1 the concerned workman was unfit to do 
his original duty i.e. to work as underground pick miner 
as per medical certificate and the Doctor recommended sur- 
face duty for him. The witness states that there was no 
demand on the part of the workman or the union to allow 
the workman to work in his original job and no light duty 
on the surface could be given to the workman, firstly, for 
the reason that there was no such job available and second- 
ly, the management was not bound to provide him with any 
such job. In para 6 of the written statement of the work- 
men it is stated that the Medical Officer, Central Hospital, 
after recovery of the workman, found him not in a position 
to perfoim the strenuous job of a pick miner and so he 
recommended for light surface duty. The statement of the 
concerned workman (WW-2) in cioss- examination is that the 
Central Hospital recommended surface duty for him. The 
statement of WW-1, the Branch Secretary of the Union, in 
cross-examination is that according to the medical certificate 
Shri Nath Mahato was unfit to work underground the mine. 
He was, however, fit to work on the surface, The witness 
states further that on the basis of the Doctor’s report the 
demand on behalf of the workman was to give him employ- 
ment on the surface of the colliery and accordingly the 
Union wanted the workman to be employed in any work on 
the surface, but theie was no work of the pick miner on 
the surface in those days. 

8. In consideration of the evidence discussed above it 
comes to this that the concerned workman Shri Nath Mahato 
was appointed to work ns pick miner underground the mine, 
that when he reported for duty on 1-7-68 after recovery from 
illness, he was unfit to work underground in his original job, 
that the Doctor found him fit to do duty at the surface and 
that the demand of the workman and also of the union 
was to allow him to work on the surface and not under- 
ground in his original job. Admittedly no job was given 
to the workman on the surface. Since the workman was 
not fit to do his original job underground the management 
cannot be said to be unjustified in not allowing him to re- 
sume work in his original job on 1-7-68 when he reported 
for duty, Again, at the relevant time there was no work of 
pick miner on the surface. The original appointment of 
the workman was not on any job on the surface and 
accordingly the management was not bound to give him 
job on the surface when be was found unfit for the work 
underground. Apart from it, there is also no reliable evi- 
dence on record to show that at the relevant time i.e. on 
1-7-68, when the workman reported for duty after recovery 
from illness, there was surface duty available which could 
be offered to the concerned workman. Tn any view of the 
case, therefore, the action of the management in stopping the 
concerned workman from work with effect from 1st July, 
1968 cannot be said to be unjustified. 

9. In view of what T have found above my answer to the 
reference is that the management of Bhowra Colliery, P, O. 
Bhowra fDhanbad) was justified in stopping Shri Nath Mahato, 
Pick Miner, from work with effect from 1st July. 1968. Tn 
that view of the matter the workman is not entitled to any 
iclicf in the present proceeding.

putes Act, 1947.

S.O. 3129. — In pursuance of section 17 of the Industrial 
Disputes Act, 1947 (14 of 1947), the Central Government 
hereby publishes the following award of the Arbitrator in 
the industrial dispute between the employers in relation to 
the Beas Dam Project, Talwara and their workmen, which 
was received by the Central Government on the 11th Octo- 
ber, 1973. 

BEFORE SFLR1 SHYAM KRISHNA, ASSISTANT LABOUR 
COMMISSIONER (CENTRAL) BAREILLY— ARBITRATOR 

Arbitration Award under Section 10A of the Industrial 
Disputes Act, 1947 in the matter of industrial dispute bet- 
ween the management of Beas Dam Project, Talwara and 
their workman represented by Workers Union Beas Dam, 
Talwara. 

Present t 

Shri Shyam Krishna, Assistant Labour Commissioner 
(Central), Bareilly (Arbitrator). 

(A) - Representing the Workers Union Beas Dam, Tal- 

wara — (1) Shri D. S. Chauhan, General Secretary, 

(B) Representing the management of Beas Dam— None. 

AWARD 

By an arbitration agreement dated 2-5-1973 published in 
the Gazette of India Part II Section 3, Sub-Seclion II as per 
Government of India in the Ministry of Laboui & Rehabi- 
litation Orders No. L. 42012/ 19/73/LR-lll, dated 8-6-1973, 
parties above named referred the following specific matters 
m the dispute to my arbitration under section 10A of the 
Industrial Disputes Act, 1947. The Agreement inter alia 
provided that the decision of the aibitrator shall be bind- 
ing on them : 

“Whether the actions of the management of Beas Dam 
Project, Talwara, District Hoshiarpur in dismissing 
Shri Prabhu Ram, Gateman from service w.e.f. 
28-4-72 and subsequently appointing him afresh in 
the same capacity w.e.f. 6-11-72 are legal and 
justified 7 If not to what relief is he entitled ?" 

The General Manager, Beas Dam Project, Talwara Town- 
ship and the General Secretary, Workers Union Beas Dam 
Project, Talwara Township, District Hoshiarpur (hereinafter 
called as 'Management’ and ‘Union’ respectively) were re- 
quested vide my letter No, B-32(2)/73, dated 11-5-73 to fur- 
nish me a self-contained statement of their case within 10 
days of the receipt of the aforesaid letter endorsing simulta- 
neously a copy thereof to the opposite party, they were fur- 
ther requested that on receipt of a copy of the statement 
from the opposite party they should furnish their comments 
thereon within 5 days of its receipt endorsing a copy simul- 
taneously to other party. The management submitted a state- 
ment of their case vide their letter No, 1 8952-54/ 125-E 
(Prabhu Ram), dated 22-5-1973. Since the Union did not 
send the statement of case, the union was once again asked 
vide my letter of even number dated 4-6-73 to send the 
statement of case endorsing a copy to the management. The 
management was also simultaneously asked to send its coun- 
ter comments thereon. As a result thereof the union sent 
its statement of a case vide letter No. 552-53/WU/D-2, 
dated 3-6-1973, and the counter comments of the manage- 
ment were sent by them vide their letter No. 32404-05/ 125-E. 
dated 17-8-73, 

The first hearing in (he arbitration proceedings was held 
on 12-7-73, Since the patties had by that time not sub- 
mitted their counter comments on each other’s stands and 
it was felt that the conclusion of arbitration proceedings will 
take some more time, the time limit for giving award by me 
was extended by two months. Subsequent hearing was fix- 
ed for 25th September 1973 vide my letter of even num- 
ber dated 13-9-73. On the request of the management for 
postponement, the date for arbitration proceedings was chang- 
ed to 1-10-73 and the parties were informed vide telegram 
dated 25-9-1973. On 1-10-73 the Union attended bid the 
management remained absent. Since the Union stressed for 
early conclusion of the arbitration proceedings and so not 
inclined for any further postponement, the hearing was pro- 
ceeded with ex-parle and concluded on 1-10-73. 


In their written statement the management have stated 
that Shri Prabhu Ram Gateman token No. 1033-B was de- 
puted at Sansarpur Railway Level Crossing in the first shift 
of 20-1-72 when he did not put the chain of the Gate of 
the said level crossing when the down Pilot passed from the 
crossing at about 1.55 A.M. and met with a Head on. colli- 
sion with E.B. 45 Which was coming from the Colony side 
after dumping coal ash in Sohan Khan. He was placed 
under summary suspension by the Supdtg. Engineer, Adminis- 
tration & Stores for obvious willful negligence in the dis- 
charge of his duties at the level crossing gate, which resulted 
in the accident involving loss of property on 20-1-72, pend- 
ing enquiry under Certified Standing Orders applicable to 
the delinquent workman, He was allowed 50 per cent wages 
as suspension allowance. The workman was found guilty 
for not putting the chain of the gate at the crossing before 
down pilot passed the same i e. just before the accident and 
not being present at the point of his duty at the crossing 
when the down Pilot crossed the level crossing and met 
with an accident with E.B. 45. He was chargcshectcd by 
the Sub-Divisional Officer. The charge was denied by the 
Workman. An Enquiry into the charges levelled against 
him, in consonence with the principle of natural justice was 
held and after observing all the formalities, the Enquiry 
Officer came to the conclusion that the workman was guilty 
of the charges levelled against him. After a careful con- 
sideration the workman was served with a Show Cause Notice 
alongwith the findings of the Enquiry Officer. When his 
reply was found unsatisfactory, he was given a second oppor- 
tunity to show cause and also an option of personal hearing 
by the Executive Engineer, Communication Division. After 
due consideration he was awarded punishment of dismissal 
from services from 28-4-72. 7 he workman preferred appeals 
to the Supdg. Engineer, Administration and Stores which was 
heard on 17-6-72. The appeal having been rejected, the 
workman appealed to the Chief Engineer Construction — the 
next higher authority, which was heard on 28-10-72 and the 
earliei decision upheld, The workman thereafter appealed 
for mercy on the pica that he had got a large family to 
support and had no means of subsistence and requested that he 
may be given some employment on compassionate grounds. 
Keeping in view the mercy appeal, he was given fresh appoint- 
ment as a Beldar against newly created post upto 30-6-73 on 
compassionate Grounds by the Chief Engineer, Construction 
which he accepted. It was further added by the manage- 
ment that the Workers Union discussed the matter mutually 
on 22-3-73 at Talwara but the issue could not be amicably 
resolved. 

Tn the counter comments No. 32404-05/125-E, dated 
17-8-73 the management raised the following preliminary 
objections : 

(i) that the instant reference of the industrial dispute to 

arbitration is without jurisdiction as there was no 
industrial dispute in existence at that time which 
was referred for adjudication. Heuee the Arbitra- 
tor has no jurisdiction to entertain, adjudicate and 
proceed with this case, 

(ii) that the workman himself accepted the fresh appoint- 

ment w.e.f. 6-11-1972 as a Beldar without any 
protest. As a request, thereof, he has ratified his 
dismissal from service w.e.f. 28-4-1972. As such 
he is now debarred to agitate for his reinstatement. 

Further in the counter comments the management stated 
that it was incorrect that Shri Prabhu Ram, Gateman was 
suspended on false charges and the charges levelled against 
him were correct, it was corroborated in the enquiry proceed- 
ings as well. Jt was also incorrect that the workman was 
dismissed wrongfully. The Union has failed lo point out 
any flaw in the enquiry proceedings which were held properly 
and the workman was afforded full opportunity to defend 
himself and examine the prosecution witnesses. The appeal 
referred by the workman^ against his dismissal was rejected 
y the Competent Authority. 

Shri Prabhu Ram was later on given a fresh appointment 
by the Chief Engineer (Construction) on compassionate 
giounds, Since the workman was held guilty of the charges 
levelled again:, l him in the Enquiry Proceedings and was 
accordingly dismissed from service, he was nol entitled to 
any wages beyond what had already been paid to him dur- 
ing the period of suspension nor reinstatement.

During hearing on 1-10-73 the Union stated that copy 
of the management’s comments on the Union’s statement 
of the case had not reached them and so asked for a copy 
thereof. The same was supplied on 1-10-73 itself. Along- 
with its counter comments, the Union filed certain documents 
to rely upon in support of their case. The Unions conten- 
tion, inter alia was that Shri Prabhu Ram Gateman was 
laced under summary suspension in a false charge on 
0-1-72. Later on he was dismissed wrongfully on 28-4-72 
and an appeal was given afresh appointment by the Chief 
Engineer. lie should therefore, be paid 50 per cent of his 
wages from 20-1-72 to 28-4-72 ns he had been paid only 
50 per cent as suspension allowance. However he should be 
reinstated from 29-4-72 on the same post with continuity of 
service and 100 per cent wages for the period 29-4-72 to 
31-12-72. 

The Union in their counter statement dated 1-10-73 point- 
ed out certain anomalies in the domestic enquiry and filed 
some document in support of the same on 1-10-73 and re- 
iterated Union’s earlier claim, 

Before commenting on the parties statements on merits, I 
feel it my duty to first deal with the preliminary objections 
made by the management in their aforesaid counter state- 
ment No. 32404-05/ 125-B, dated 17-8-73 reproduced be- 
low : — 

(i) that the instant reference of the industrial dispute to 

arbitration is without jurisdiction as there was no 
industrial dispute in existence at that time which 
was referred for adjudication. Hence the Arbitra- 
tor has no jurisdiction to entertain, adjudicate and 
proceed with this case. 

(ii) that the workman himself accepted the fresh appoint- 

ment w.e.f. 6-11-1972 as a Beldar without any pro- 
test. As a result thereof, he has ratified his dis- 
missal from service w.e.f. 28-4-1972. As such he 
is now debarred to agitate for his reinstatement. 

So far as the first objection is concerned after careful 
examination, it is over-ruled for the reasons that objection 
is vague and not clear; the industrial dispute was in existence 
at the time of referring the issue for my arbitration. 

As regard the second objection, it is also over-ruled for 
the reasons that the lodging of protest just at the time of 
getting appointment by the worker when he be in miserable 
need of it, is not necessary. The worker could very well 
lodge the protest and file the claim even subsequently of 
course in reasonable time — which he very much did through 
the Workers Union in the shape of discussions with the 
management and the industrial dispute under reference. 


Now coming to merits of the claim of the workman I have 
to answer the following points ; — 

(i) Whether the dismissal of Shri Prabhu Ram, Gate- 

man from service w.e.f, 28-4-72 was justified. 

(ii) Whether reappointment afresh of Shri Prabhu Ram 

w.e.f. 6-11-72 was justified? 

As per point No. (1) is concerned, after carefully examin- 
ing the view point, contention and arguments of the parties 
I feel that the dismissal was not in order and justified- for 
the following reasons : — 

(1) A charge-sheeting authority cannot be a Enquiry 

Officer in the same case as he happens to be a in- 
terested person. 

(2) The Enquiry Officer has gone beyond the perview 

of the responsibility assigned to him in manage- 
ment’s endorsement No. 510-12/SE-I1, dated 
12-2-72 by enquiring into subsequent chargesheet 
issued to the workman under No. 548-50/86, dated 
17-2-72. He was supposed to restrict enquiry only 
into the chargesheet No. 435-36-5-E-II, dated 
5-2-72. 

(3) Too much reliance has been made on the statement 

of Shri Kewal Krishna, Loco Driver and Shri Prem 
Singh. Euclid Operator. Since Shri Prem Singh, 
Euclid Operator of E.B. 45 was never produced as 
Prosecution Witness nor his statement recorded 
during the course of enquiry in the presence of the 
accused, il is not understood how his statement 
was recorded, how can it form a part of the do- 
mestic enquiry. As regards the statement of Shri 
Kewal Krishna, Loco Driver the same should not 
have been relied upon by the Enquiry Officer as 
he himself had been involved in the accident and 
was a highly interested witness to save his own 
skins at the cost of others. 

As regards the second issue of reappointment afresh I am 
of the view that in view of aforesaid that the dismissal was 
not justified, the question of reappointment afresh does not 
arise. 

I, therefore, hold that the actions of the management In 
dismissing Shri Prabhu Ram, Gateman from service w.e.f. 
28-4-72 and subsequently appointing him afresh in the same 
capacity w.e.f. 6-11-72 arc. not legal and justified, and as 
such the worker is entitled for reinstatement with continuity 
of service and back wages and allied benefits w.e.f. 28-4-72 
and award accordingly.
