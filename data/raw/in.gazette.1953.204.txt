New Delhi, the 22nd October 1953 

S.R.O. 1991. — In exercise of the powers conferred by clause (1) of article 299 
at the Constitution, the President hereby directs that the following further 
amendments shall be made in the Notification of the Government of India in the 
Ministry of Law No. S.R.O. 215, dated the 9th February, 1952, relating to the 
execution of contracts and assurances of property, namely: — 

1. In Part I of the said Notification, after the existing proviso, the following 

further proviso shall be added, namely: — I 

“Provided further that in the case of an Indian mission or post in a foreign 
country , such contracts and instruments may also be executed by the 
Head of the mission or post. 

Explanation. — The term “Head of a mission or post” includes a High Com- 
missioner, a Deputy High Commissioner, a Commissioner, an 
Ambassador, a Minister, a Representative, a Charge d’ Affairs, a 
Consul-General and a Consul.” 

2. In Part X of the said Notification, in item 1, under Head D, after entry (ii), 
the following entry shall be added, namely: — 

"(iii) All agreements or leases of hire of buildings, lands and other 
immovable property; by the Agricultural Marketing Adviser to the 
Government of India." 

3. In Part XX of the said Notification, under Head B, in item 2, after entry 
(vi), the following entry shall be added, namely: — 

"(vii) Contracts and other instruments relating to services rendered and 
supplies made to the Kandla Port Administration — (a) where the 
amount or value does not exceed Rs. 20,000; by Executive Engineer 
and (b) where the amount or value does not exceed Rs. 2,00,000; by 
the Superintending Engineer.” 

[No. F.32-III/52-L.]

ACT NO. XXII OF 1867 

An Act for the regulation of public Sarais and Puraos. 

Preamble. — Whereas it Is expedient to provide for the regulation of public 
Sarais and Puraos: It Is hereby enacted as follows: — 

2. Interpretation clause. — In this Act, unless there be something repugnant in 
the subject or context, — 

“Sarai” means any building used for the shelter and accommodation of travellers, 
and includes, in any case In which only part of a building is used as a sarai, the 
part so used of such building. It also includes a purao so far as the provisions 
of this Act are applicable thereto: 

“Keeper of a sarai”. — "Keeper of a sarai" includes the owner and any person 
having or acting in the care or management thereof: 

“Magistrate of the District”, — “Magistrate of the District” means the chief 
officer charged with the executive administration of a district in criminal matters 
whatever may be his designation, 

3. Notice of this Act to be given to keepers of sarais, — Within six months after 
this Act shall come into operation, the Magistrate of the District in which any 
sarai to which this Act shall apply may be situated shall, and from time to time

thereafter such Magistrate may, give to the keeper of every such sarai notice in 
writing of this Act, by leaving such notice for the keeper at the sarai; and shall 
by such notice require the keeper to register the sarai as by this Act provided. 

Such notice may be in the form in the Schedule to this Act annexed or to the 
like effect. 

4. Registers of sarais to be kept. — The Magistrate of the District shall keep a 
register in which shall be entered by such Magistrate or such other person as he 
shall appoint in this behalf, the names and residences of the keepers ' of" all sarais 
within his jurisdiction, and the situation of every such sarai. 

No charge shall be made for making any such entry. 

5. Lodgers, etc., not to be received In sarais until registered. — After one month 
after the giving of such notice to register as by this Act provided, the keeper of 
any sarai or any other person shall not receive any lodger or allow any person, 
cattle, sheep, elephant, camel or other animal, or any vehicle, to halt or be placed 
in such sarai until the same and the name and residence of the keeper thereof 
shall have been registered as by this Act provided. 

6. Magistrate may refuse to register keeper not producing certificate of. 
character. — The Magistrate of the District may, if he shall think fit, refuse to register 
as the keeper of a sarai a person who does not produce a certificate of character 
in such form and signed by such person as the State Government shall from time 
to time direct. 

7. Duties of keepers of sarais. — The keeper of a sarai shall be bound — 

(1) when any person in such sarai is ill of any infectious or contagious 

disease, or dies of such disease, to give immediate notice thereof to 
the nearest police station: 

(2) at all times when required by any Magistrate or any other person duly 

authorized by the Magistrate of the District in this behalf, to give 
him free access to the sarai and allow him to inspect the same or any 
part thereof: 

(3) to thoroughly cleanse the rooms and verandahs, and drains. of the sarai, 

and the wells, tanks, or other sources from which wate'r is obtained for 
the persons or animals using it, to the satisfaction of, and so often 
as shall be required by, the Magistrate of the "District, or such person 
as he shall appoint In this behalf: 

(4) to remove all noxious vegetation on or near the sarai, and ail trees and 

branches of trees capable of affording to thieves means of entering 
or leaving the sarai: 

(5) to keep the gates, walls, fences, roofs and drains of the sarai In repair: 

(6) to provide such number of watchmen as may, in the opinion of the 

Magistrate of the District, subject to such rules as the Chief Commis- 
sioner may prescribe in this behalf, be necessary for the safety and 
protection of persons and animals or vehicles lodging In, halting at 
or placed in the sarai: and 

(7) to exhibit a list of charges for the use of the sarai at such place #nd i n 

such form and languages as the Magistrate of the District shall from 
time to time direct. 

8. Power to order reports from keepers of sarais. — The keeper pf a sarai shall 
from time to time, if required so to do by an order of the Magistrate of the 
District served upon him, report, either orally or in writing as may be directed by 
the Magistrate to such Magistrate or to such person as the Magistrate shall appoint, 
every person who resorted to such sarai during the preceding day or night. 

If written reports are required for any space of time exceeding^ a single day or 
night, schedules shall be furnished by the Magistrate of the District to the keeper. 

The keeper shall from time to time fill up the said schedules with the informa- 
tion so required, and transmit them to the said Magistrate, in such manner and at 
such intervals as may from time to time be ordered by him. 

9. Power to shut up, secure, clear and clean deserted sarais. — If any sarai by 
reason of abandonment or of disputed ownership shall remain untenanted, and 
thereby become a resort of idle and disorderly persons, or become in a filthy or 
unwholesome state, or be complained of by any two or more of the neighbours as a 
nuisance, the Magistrate of the District, after due enquiry, "may cause notice In 
writing to be given to the owner or to the person claiming to be the owner, If he

put on some conspicuous part of the sarai, requiring the persons concerned therein,

and if such requisition shall not be complied with within eight days, the 
Magistrate of the District may cause the necessary work to be executed, and all 
expenses thereby incurred shall be paid by the owner of the sarai, and shall be 
recoverable like penalties under this Act, or. in case of abandonment or disputed 
ownership of the sarai, by the sale of any material found therein. 

10. Taking down or repairing ruinous sarais. — If a sarai or any part thereof be 
deemed by the Magistrate of the District to be in a ruinous state, or likely Lo 
fall, or in any way dangerous to the persons or animals lodging in or halting at 
the sarai, he. shall give notice in writing to the keeper of the sarai requiring him 
forthwith to take down, repair or secure (as the case may be) the sarai or such 
part thereof as the case may require. 

If the keeper do not begin to take down, repair or secure the sarai, or such part 
as aforesaid within three days after such notice, and' complete such work with due 
diligence, the Magistrate shall cause all or so much of the sarai as he shall think 
necessary to be taken down, repaired or otherwise secured. 

All the expenses so incurred by the Magistrate shall be paid by the keeper of 
the sarai, and shall be recoverable from him as hereinafter mentioned. 

11. Sale of materials of ruinous sarais, — If any such sarai or any part thereof 
be taken down by virtue of the powers aforesaid, the Magistrate of the District 
may sell the materials thereof, or so much of the same as shall be taken 
down under the provisions of the last preceding section, and apply the. proceeds 
of such sale in payment of the expenses incurred, and shall restore the over-plus 
(if any) arising from such sale to the owner of such satai on demand, and may 
recover the deficiency (if any) as if the amount thereof were a penalty under this 
Act, 

12. Penalty for permitting sarais to be filthy or overgrown. — Whoever, being the 
keeper of any sarai, suffers the same to be in a filthy and unwholesome state, or 
overgrown with vegetation, or after the expiration of two days from the time of 
his receiving notice in writing from the Magistrate of the District to cleanse or 
clear the same, or after he shall have been convicted of suffering the same to be in 
such a stale 'or so overgrown as aforesaid, shall allow the same to continue in 
such state, or so overgrown, shall be liable to the penalties provided in section 14 
of this Act: 

Proviso. — 'Provided that the Magistrate of the District may, in lieu of enforcing 
such daily penalty, enter on and cleanse or clear the said sarai, and the expense 
incurred by the Magistrate in respect (hereof shall be paid to him by the keeper, 
and shall be recoverable as by this Act provided in the case of penalties. 

13. Power for Chief Commissioner to make regulations. — The Chief Commis- 
sioner may from time to time make regulations for the better attainment of the 
objects of this Act, provided that such rules be not Inconsistent with this Act or 
with any other law for the time being In force, and may from time to time repeal, 
alter and add to the same. 

All regulations made under this Act and all repeals thereof, and alterations 
and additions thereto, shall be published in the Official Gazette. 

14. Penalty for infringing Act or regulations. — If the keeper of a sarai offend 
agaipst any of the provisions of this Act or any of the regulations made in pursuance 
of this Act, he shall for every such offence be liable on conviction before any 
Magistrate to a penalty not exceeding twenty rupees, and to a further penalty not 
exceeding one rupee a day for every day during which the offence continues: 

Provided always that this Act shall not exempt any person from any penalty or 
other liability to which he may be subject, irrespective of this Act. 

All penalties imposed under this Act may be recovered in the same manner 
as fines may be recovered under the laws in force In Manipur relating to the 
recovery of fines. 

15 . Conviction for third offence to disqualify persons from keeping sarais,— Wher" 
a keeper of a sarai is convicted of a third offence under this Act, he shall not 
afterwards act as keeper of a sarai without the license in writing of the Magistrate 
of the District, who may cither withhold such license or grant the same on such 
terms and conditions as he may think fit.

17. Extent of Act.— This Act shall in the first instance extend only to the terri- 
tories under the Government of the Lieutenant-Governor of the North-Western 
Provinces of the Presidency of Fort William in Bengal. 

Power to Chief Commissioner to extend this Act. — But It shall be lawful for

the towns of Calcutta, Madras and Bombay. 

18. Short title, — This Act may be called the Sarais Act, 1867.

Application No. 123 op 1953 
(Arising out of Reference No. 6 of 1952)

Shri D. Narsingh, Chief Personnel Officer, M/s. Tata Iron & Steel Co. Ltd.,

AWARD 

This is an application under Section 33A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947. 

2. The applicant alleges that she was discharged from service from 1st January 
1953 without the express permission of the Tribunal in writing in spite of pendency 
of Reference No. 6 of 1952 and hence she has filed the present application. The 
case of the opposite party is that the applicant was made to retire from service 
from 1st January 1953, as she had attained the age of 60 years, which is the age 
of superannuation under their Retiring Gratuity Rules. It was alleged that the 
applicant had been got examined by the Colliery Medical Officer, who certified 
that the applicant was above sixty years of age and she was therefore served 
with a notice and superannuated from 1st January 1963. 

3. It is an admitted fact that the applicant’s services were terminated from 
1st January 1953 at a time when Reference No. 6 of 1952 to which the opposite 
party and their workmen are parties was pending before the Tribunal, The 
opposite party’s case is that they had not in any way altered the conditions of 
service applicable to the applicant, because under the terms of service, a person, 
on attaining the age of sixty years, would be superannuated and must retire. 
For this, reliance has been placed on the retiring Gratuity Rules of the company, 
which has been produced at Exhibit 16. These rules were framed in 1937. It 
appears that these rules were made in the first instance for the main factory of 
the Tata Iron and Steel Co. Ltd., at Jamshedpur. Mr. Engineer, Exhibit 21, who 
is Deputy Superintendent of Collieries of the company, has stated that the scheme 
of Retiring Gratuity was introduced in the collieries about 10 years ago. He could 
not remember the exact year but that it was after 1937. Now so far as the appli- 
cant is concerned, it is an admitted fact that she has been in service of the com- 
pany from 1933. In other words, when she entered service, the Gratuity Rules 
were not in force. Mr, Engineer has admitted that excepting for a rule contained 
in the Gratuity Rules, there is no rule that a workman must retire on attaining 
the age of 60 years, He has also admitted that for people who joined service 
before 1937, there was no rule or agreement about their retiring at a particular 
age and the practice was that persons continued to work till they were found 
physically unfit. In other words, at the time when the applicant joined Service 
in 1933, there was no condition of service that she would have to retire when she 
completed the age of 60 years. On the contrary, the rule was that she may 
continue in service as long as she was physically fit to work. 



Part II— Sec. 3] THE GAZETTE OF INDIA, OCTOBER 31, 1953 1827 


4. It is true that retiring gratuity rules contain 'a rule (Rule 5) which lays 
down that every uncovenantcd employee of the company must retire from uervice 
on attaining the age of sixty years (unless his service is extended as mentioned 
therein) . m my opinion, this rule cannot afl’ect a person who had already joined 
the service of the company before the rules were framed or introduced. By 
framing a set of rules, an employer could not change or prejudice the terms of 
service of an employee who was already in service before that date. The 
employee in service should ordinarily be given the option to elect whether he 
should accept the new rules or not. There is nothing on record to show that 
when the gratuity scheme was introduced, the workmen were even Informed 
about it, much less given an option to elect the rules or not. Mr. Engineer stated 
that he was not sure whether a notice was put up on the notice board about the 
introduction of the gratuity scheme. It may also be noted that though the rules 
were framed in 1937 and were applied to the collieries in about 1943 or there- 
abouts, the rule about the superannuation was not enforced till 1952. In that 
year, the management started issuing notices to the employees asking them 
whether they accepted the ages mentioned in their service card as correct. This 
would also show that till 1952 the old workmen may not be aware of the company 
having framed a rule that all the employees must retire on attaining the age of 
60 years. It may lastly be noted that even under the above Rules, no employee 
was or is entitled as a right to any payment of gratuity. In other words, though 
the company had framed rules for gratuity, the rules are such as could not be 
enforced by an employee so as to claim gratuity on retirement. 

5. It was argued that in about 1949, the management were thinking of dropping 
the gratuity scheme in the case of employees getting a salary of Rs. 300 per month 
or less and thereupon the General Secretary of the Tata Workers’ Union raised a 
protest and ultimately the management dropped the idea of withdrawing the 
scheme. From this, it was urged that it must be presumed that the workmen knew 
about the gratuity scheme. I am unable to agree with this contention. The above 
facts would only show that the Union leaders knew that there was a gratuity- 
scheme. It did not necessarily mean that the workmen knew about it. At best, it 
might be said that the members of the Tata Workers Union may have been 
knowing about this scheme; but there is nothing to show that the applicant was 
a member of that union or that she knew about it. I might repeat that she was 
in employment from 1933 and could not be bound by any new rules framed, unless 
she was informed about it and she elected to be governed by the new rules. Here 
not only did she not elect to be governed by Eratuity scheme, but she did not 
even know about it. 

6. As I said above, Mr. Engineer has admitted that excepting for the above 
rule in the gratuity rules, there is no rule that a workman must retire on attain- 
ing the age of 60 years. The applicant was not bound by the above rule and she 
cannot therefore be forced to retire on attaining the age of 60 years. As admit- 
ted by Mr. Engineer, workmen who joined service before 1937 continued to work 
till they were found physically unfit and hence so far as the applicant is concern- 
ed, she cannot be made to retire as long as she is physically fit. Admittedly she 
is physically fit. Even the certificate given by the colliery Medical Officer shows 
that she is physically fit. That being so, the order of the management forcing her 
to retire from 1st January 1953 on the ground that she had attained the age of 60 
years was bad and must be set aside, 

7. I may mention here that the applicant contended that she was not 60 years 
of age and she was only 43. In this connection, she relied on a medical certificate 
granted by the Assistant Civil Surgeon, Dhanbad, who certified that she was about 
50 years old. On the other hand, the colliery Medical Officer certified that she was 
about 60 years of age when she was examined on 6th August 1952. She complain- 
ed that she had not been properly examined and later on she produced the above 
certificate and thereupon the management got her examined by a Medical Board 
consisting of three of their medical officers and this Medical Board also found that 
she was above sixty, Ordinarily the Tribunal would not interfere in a decision of 
the management when they had evidence bfore them to show that the age of a 
person was sixty years, unless it was shown that their action was not bona fide. 
When there is a conflict of medical evidence, it is for the management to decide 
which evidence they should accept. In the present case there is nothing to show that 
the management were guided by improper motives or that their above decision was 
not bona fide. There arc however one or two irregularities. The first is about the 
constitution of the Medical Board. One of the members of the Medical Board was a 
subordinate of the doctor who had already given a certificate that the applicant 
was sixty years of age. It further appears that not only the applicant’s case but 
two other cases were referred at the same time to the same Medical Board and one 
Medical Officer who had already given a particular opinion about one of these

other persons was also a member of the Medical Board; though it was because 
that person was aggrieved by that Medical Officer’s opinion that the matter was

appears that as against the company’s medical officer’s opinion, the applicant had

AWARD 

This is an application purporting to be one under Section 33A of the Indus- 
trial Disputes Act, 1947. 

2. The application was filed bv one Mr. G. R. Panda who described himself as 
the General Secretary. Colliery Mazdoor Sangh, Kumarbazar Jubak Samity. He 
filed the present application stating that during the pendency of the dispute 
regarding Republic Day and Independence Day holidays, the management of the 
Mundalpoor Colliery had illegally and unjustifiably declared a lock-out and dis- 
missed about 2,600 workmen. 

3. The opposite party raised two preliminary objections. Firstly, it contendec 
that no dispute or proceeding between the management and its ‘workmen war 
pending on 10th June 1952 because the reference regarding paid holidays was 
disp^ed of by the Labour Appellate Tribunal on 4th April 1952. Secondly, it 
contended that the application was not maintainable in law, as it was not made 
by a workman, aggrieved by the action of the management. 



Part II— Sec. 3] THE GAZETTE Of INDIA, OCTOBER 31, 1953 1829 


4. It may be noted that a reference about paid holidays was first made by the 
Government to this Tribunal on 12th April 1951. My learned predecessor decided 
it on 3rd August 1951. The matter went in appeal, and the appeal was decided 
on 4th April 1952. Thereafter, however, the Government made a fresh reference 
to this Tribunal on 5th May 1952 and the same was registered in this Tribunal 
as Reference No. 6 of 1952. This reference was pending on 10th June 1952 and 
if any action was taken by the management on that day, it certainly would 
amount to contravention of Section 33 of the Industrial Disputes Act and would 
entitle an employee aggrieved by such contravention to make a complaint in 
writing about it "under Section 33A of the Industrial Disputes Act. The first 
objection therefore cannot be accepted. 

5. The second objection against this application is that it is not made by or 
on behalf of an employee aggrieved by the contravention of Section 33 by the 
management. It has been alleged that the management dismissed about 2,500 
workmen on 10th June 1952. If that was so, it would have been open to these 
workmen, or any of them, to make a complaint under Section 33A of the Indus- 
trial Disputes Act. No such complaint has been made by any of the workmen. 
Mr. Panda who filed the present application, does not appear to have been 
authorised by any workman or workmen to file the present application on his or 
their behalf. A trade union or a person other than the aggrieved workmen would 
not be entitled to make a complaint under Section 33A. Mr. Paul who appeared 
for the applicant fairly conceded this. 

6. The result is that the application as framed is not maintainable and must 
be dismissed- I pass my award accordingly.

CENTRAL GOVERNMENT INDUSTRIAL TRIBUNAL AT DHANBAD 
Application No. 44 of 1953 
(Arising out of Reference No. 6 of 1952) 

In the matter of an application U/S 33A of Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, 

Present:

Parties: 

Purusottam M. Parmar, represented by Secretary, Chotanagpur Coalfield 
Workers Union, P.O. Bermo, Dist. Hazaribagh — Complainant. 

Versus 

The management of Kalyani Selected Kargali Colliery, P.O. Bermo, Dist. 
Hazaribagh— Opposite Party. 


Appearances: 

Shri Mithilesh Kumar Sinha, Secretary, Chotanagpur Coalfield Workers 
Union, representing Purusottam M. Parmar, P.O. Bermo, Dist. Hazari- 
bagh — For Complainant. 

Shri L. N. Singh, c/o Kalyani Selected Kargali Colliery, P.O. Bermo, Dist. 
Hazaribagh — For the Opposite Party. 

AWARD 

This is an application under Section 33A of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947. 

2. This complaint was filed by Shri Mithilesh Kumar Sinha, General Secretary 
of the Chotanagpur Coalfield Workers Union, representing Purusottam M. Parmar 
who was an employee of the opposite party. He alleged that the opposite party 
illegally and wrongfully removed Shri Parmar, who was Underground and Surface 
in Charge from 28th March 1953, without the permission of this Tribunal during 
the pendency of Rerference No. 6 of 1952 relating to paid holidays. 



i 830 TEE GAZETTE OF INDIA, OCTOBER 31, 19ffe [Part !l— Sec. 3 


3. The opposite party contended that Parmar was not a workman; that the 
opposite party had not illegally removed him from service; that Parmar was 
employed temporarily and as his services were' no longer required because the 
quarry was being closed, he was discharged; and that he had also committed 
certain bad acts and was guilty of dishonesty. It was therefore prayed that the 
application should be dismissed. 

4. At the hearing before me, Mr. M. K. Sinha was not present but Mr. Parmar 
was present in person. Admittedly he was appointed by the opposite party as 
a temporary quarry and underground in charge. Mr. Parmar admitted before me 
that his duties as a quarry and Underground in Charge were purely supervisory. 
He had not to do any clerical or manual work and could not therefore be said 
to be a workman within the definition given under the Industrial Disputes Act. 
That being so, the present application would not lie, 

5. The second objection against the maintainability of this application Is that 
Mr. Parmar was appointed only temporarily. The order of appointment dated 
0th January 1953 clearly shows that he was appointed as temporary quarry and 
Underground in Charge. Below this appointment letter, he has made an endorse- 
ment that he accepted the temporary appointment as quarry and Underground 
in Charge. He was discharged on 28th March 1953, that is within three months 
of his appointment, A person who was appointed on a purely temporary basis 
could be discharged by the employer without notice or without giving any reasons. 
In their notice of discharge, the management stated that they had not found his 
work satisfactory and also that they were going to close the overburden work 
in quarry No. 1 and for both these reasons, he was discharged. The allegation 
that the quarry was being closed has not been denied on behalf of the applicant. 
In the circumstances, the opposite party was entitled, when they were closing 
their quarry, to discharge a person whom they engaged temporarily. 

6. It was contended on behalf of the opposite party that the present applica- 
tion was not maintainable as it was not filed by the aggrieved workman but was 
filed by the Secretary of the Chotanagpur Coalfield Workers Union, whereas an 
application under Section 33A of the Act ought to be made by the aggrieved 
employee himself. It is true that an application under Section 33A must be made 
by the aggrieved workman and that it cannot be made by anyone else. In the 
present case, the complaint is made by Mr. Sinha. He has stated therein that he 
was making it as representing Mr. Parmar. He had also filed a letter of authority 
signed by Mr. Parmar authorising Mr. Sinha to make the application and to do 
all other acts in connection with this application. I think that in substance, It is 
an application which can be taken to have been made by or on behalf of 
Mr. Parmar, and I would not dismiss it on this technical ground, It cannot how- 
ever be grunted, firstly because Mr. Parmar was not a workman as defined in the 
Industrial Disputes Act, and secondly because ho was engaged temporarily for 
a temporary work and was discharged as the temporary work was being closed 
and also as his work was not found satisfactory. 

In the result, the present application fails. The applicant is not entitled to any 
relief. I pass my award accordingly. 


The 8th October 1953. 


(Sd.) L, P. Dave, Chairman.
