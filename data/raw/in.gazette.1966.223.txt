Complaint No. 7 of 1965 

M/s. Jaipur Udyog Limited, Sawai Madhopur — 

Complainants 

versus 

The Union of India owning the Western Railway and 
represented by ils General Manager, Bombay — 

Respondent. 

Whereas the complainants above-named have filed a 
complaint stating that they own a cement factory at 
Sawai Madhopur, a station on the Western Railway, that 


the sidings inside the factory premises were constructed 
by the complainants and are maintained at their own 1 
cost, that, as a result of an assurance by the respondent 
railway in or about 1958 that the complainants would 
not be adversely affected financially or otherwise, the 
complainants relaid their sidings and also constructed a 
marshalling yard at a cost of about Rs. 10 lakhs, that, 
despite the assurance by the respondent and despite less 
work for the respondent railway administration conse- 
quent on re-modelling, in view of the transfer points 
having been shifted nearer to railway area, the respon- 
dent has considerably increased the siding charges from 
time to time, that the shunting work the respondent now 
does for the complainants is nil or very much less than 
before, that the respondent has a duty to afford all 
reasonable facilities for dealing with the Jraffic,, and that 
the lovy of siding charges and maintenance charges is un- 
reasonable. 

That the respondent does not levy any siding charges 
in respect of the traffic of Lakkeri Cement Factory and 
Savalia Cement Factory both situated near the complain- 
ants’ factory, that whereas the complainants have pro- 
vided transfer lines at their own cost, the said two fac- 
tories had declined to do so, in spite of which they are 
not charged any siding charges, that this amounts to 
undue and unreasonable preference and advantage to the 
said two factories in clear violation of Section 28 of the 
Act; 

And whereas the complainants have prayed for (1) 
directing the respondent not to levy any placement or 
siding charges or maintenance charges in respect of the 
complainants’ traffic at Sawai Madhopur, (2) or, fixa- 
tion by the Tribunal of reasonable siding and/or main- 
tenance charges; (3) directing the respondent to refund 
the excess charges collected, (4) directing the respondent 
to abate the discrimination against the complainants 
vis-a-vis the other two cement factories, (5) giving effect 
to the reliefs from the date of the complaint and, (6) 
awarding costs to the complainants. 

And whereas it is thought that there xuay be persons 
who are not on record but have the same interest in the 
proceedings as the complainants or respondent above- 

named.
